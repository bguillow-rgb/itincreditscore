## Do ITIN holders actually have a credit file to check?

All three major bureaus, Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, can generate a credit file for an ITIN holder. Whether a score exists depends entirely on what's in that file.

If a bureau can't match enough credit activity to your ITIN, you may get a "thin file" notice instead of a number. Once you start establishing regular payments, like rent, utilities, or credit-builder loans, each bureau is more likely to generate a usable score, though the exact algorithm and timing differ across the three agencies.

The practical threshold: most scoring models, both FICO and VantageScore, require at least one account with six or more months of reported payment history before a score can be calculated. If you haven't opened a reporting account yet, no monitoring service will show you a number. For guidance on building that first account, see our guide to [credit builder loans with an ITIN](/articles/credit-builder-loan-with-itin) or [how to build credit with an ITIN number](/articles/how-to-build-credit-with-itin-number).

According to the CFPB's June 2025 report on credit invisibles, about 12.5% of U.S. adults either have no credit record at all (2.7%) or have a record without a scoreable file (9.8%). ITIN holders are disproportionately represented in that group, simply because fewer lenders historically accepted ITINs for reporting. That gap is closing, but it means the first step for many ITIN holders isn't "check my score" but "build my file." Both steps are covered here.

## The accounts-suspended problem: why does this keep happening to ITIN holders?

*A question we hear often:* why does every service flag my account immediately after signup?

Services that accept ITINs share a consistent pattern: automated suspensions, one-call fixes, and odd verification quirks. Every method that works goes through some version of this gauntlet. What you actually get at the end, VantageScore 3.0, FICO 8, FICO 9, a full credit file, alerts, depends on small details that determine whether you've wasted a week on something that doesn't work.

The underlying cause is structural. Automated fraud-detection systems at bureaus and third-party monitoring tools are trained on SSN patterns. ITINs begin with the digit 9 and follow a different structure, which trips a flag. This is not a rejection of your file or your identity. It's a false positive from a system that wasn't designed with ITIN holders in mind.

The fix is almost always a single phone call. Reach a human agent, answer a few knowledge-based questions about your credit file (account counts, approximate limits, open dates), and the suspension gets lifted. This typically takes fewer than 10 minutes. At myFICO specifically, if your account is suspended right after signup (which is common), one call to their support line resolves it after a few security questions about your credit file. One call, under 10 minutes.

4. If online verification fails, Experian's mail-based verification is available. Their online upload form lets you submit documentation, with your report mailed in 7-12 days.
5. For phone assistance, call Experian at (888) 397-3742.

What you get: FICO Score 8 (Experian data), full Experian credit report, daily updates, and score factor explanations. Cost: free, no credit card required.

## Method 5: Direct bureau mail request (free, report only, all three bureaus)

*A question we hear often:* what if all the online methods fail to verify my identity?

The mail request is your guaranteed fallback. Every person in the United States, regardless of whether they have an SSN or an ITIN, has the right to request their credit report from all three major bureaus. That right extends to ITIN holders. Under federal law, everyone is entitled to a free copy of their credit report each year from each bureau.

You cannot get your credit score or credit report at AnnualCreditReport.com with an ITIN via the online portal. You can, however, request a free credit report from each bureau by mailing the required documents.

What to include in a mail request:
- Full legal name and current address
- Date of birth
- ITIN (written clearly)
- Government-issued photo ID (copy of passport, driver's license, or consular ID)
- One proof of address (utility bill, lease, or bank statement)

Your credit report will arrive by mail within 15 days. You'll receive a detailed report, not a numeric score. But the report is the foundation of everything: it shows which accounts are being reported, lets you check for errors, and helps you prepare for disputes. ## How do FICO scores and VantageScores differ for ITIN holders?

*This one comes up a lot:* readers are confused when their Credit Karma VantageScore looks different from the FICO score their lender pulls.

Both FICO and VantageScore use the same 300-850 range and the same five core factors: payment history, amounts owed, length of credit history, new credit, and credit mix. The weighting differs, and the two models treat thin files differently. Your scores from each model may vary by 20-40 points, and for ITIN holders with newer or thinner files, that gap can be even wider. VantageScore can generate a score with as little as one month of history; FICO requires six months.

| Feature | FICO Score 8 | VantageScore 3.0 |
|---|---|---|
| Minimum history to score | 6 months, 1 account | 1-2 months, 1 account |
| Used by top lenders | ~90% | Less common (monitoring tools) |
| Available free with ITIN | Experian.com, myFICO free plan, CreditWise | Credit Karma, myEquifax Core Credit |
| Score range | 300-850 | 300-850 |
| Treats paid collections as | Still counts against score | Reduced impact |

The practical takeaway: if you're a newer ITIN holder, VantageScore may show you a score before FICO can, which is useful for tracking early progress. Once your file reaches the six-month mark, shift your attention to monitoring your FICO Score. That's the number that matters for most lending decisions. ## What if I check my score and get "no score available"?

*Readers frequently ask:* I signed up for a service and it says I have a file but no score. What does that mean?

If you've established some credit history, you'll likely see a numeric score. If you haven't, the bureau may return a "no score available" message or a limited view reflecting only the few accounts it can locate.

"No score" is not a rejection. It's not a problem with your ITIN. It means your file doesn't yet meet the minimum threshold for the scoring model in use. For FICO Score 8, that minimum is one account open for at least six months and reported within the last six months. For VantageScore 3.0, the bar is lower, usually one to two months of history.

The path forward: open one account that reports to the bureau where you got "no score," keep it in good standing, and wait six months. A [credit builder loan with an ITIN](/articles/credit-builder-loan-with-itin) is one of the most reliable ways to build that history deliberately. For a full discussion of thin files, see our guide on [thin credit file with an ITIN](/articles/thin-credit-file-itin-holder).

## How often should I check my ITIN credit score?

There's no limit to how often you check your own score. All monitoring methods are soft pulls, which don't affect your credit score. Only hard pulls, made when you apply for credit, can lower your score temporarily.

A practical monitoring routine for ITIN holders:

- Monthly: review your FICO Score 8 from myFICO's free plan or Experian.com and note the top score factors.
- Every few days (or set up alerts): Credit Karma's live alerts cover Equifax and TransUnion VantageScores and flag sudden changes quickly.
- Quarterly: pull a full credit report from one bureau in rotation. With three bureaus and Equifax's current settlement offering up to seven free reports per year through 2026, you can maintain regular report access at no cost.
- Before any major credit application: check all three bureaus at least 30-60 days in advance so you have time to dispute errors before a lender pulls your file.

Understanding why your score might show different numbers at different services is covered in our guide on [why your credit score differs by bureau with an ITIN](/articles/why-credit-score-different-each-bureau-itin).

## FAQs

Can I check my credit score with an ITIN instead of an SSN?

Yes. Several free and paid services accept an ITIN for identity verification, including Experian.com, myEquifax Core Credit, myFICO's free plan, and Capital One CreditWise. Each pulls your score as a soft inquiry, so checking never lowers your score.

Does checking my credit score with an ITIN hurt my score?

No. Checking is always a soft pull. Only hard pulls, made when you apply for credit, can lower your score temporarily.

Does NerdWallet work with an ITIN for credit score access?

No. NerdWallet's verification method with TransUnion is tied to a Social Security Number and cannot accept an ITIN at this time. Use Experian.com or Credit Karma instead.

Can I use AnnualCreditReport.com with an ITIN?

The online portal generally requires an SSN. You can request a free credit report from each of the three bureaus using an ITIN by mailing the required documents.

What score model do I get when I check with an ITIN?

It depends on the service. Experian.com and myFICO deliver a FICO Score 8. Equifax Core Credit and Credit Karma provide a VantageScore 3.0. Capital One CreditWise provides a FICO Score 8 based on TransUnion data.

What is the fastest way to see a FICO score with an ITIN?

Sign up for myFICO's free plan online. It accepts ITIN signups and provides your Equifax FICO Score 8 and complete credit file.

Why do I get an "account suspended" message when signing up with my ITIN?

Automated fraud filters flag ITINs because they differ structurally from SSN patterns. It's a common problem and a fixable one. An automated suspension after signup is resolvable in one phone call to the bureau.

How many bureaus will have a credit file for me as an ITIN holder?

All three major bureaus, Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, can hold a credit file tied to your ITIN. Your file may appear at one or two bureaus before all three, depending on where your creditors report.

Do I need to have credit history before I can check my score?

You need at least one account with six or more months of reporting before most FICO models can generate a score. If you're below that threshold, expect a "no score available" message.

Can I check scores from all three bureaus for free with an ITIN?

Yes, by combining three free services: Experian.com (Experian FICO 8), myEquifax Core Credit (Equifax VantageScore 3.0), and Capital One CreditWise (TransUnion FICO 8). Together they give you free, ongoing three-bureau coverage with no SSN required.
