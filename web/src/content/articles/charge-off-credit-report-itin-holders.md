A charge-off showing up on your credit report can feel like a financial emergency, especially when you have spent months or years building a credit history with your ITIN. This guide explains what a charge-off is, how it lands on your ITIN credit file, what it does to your score, and what realistic recovery looks like.

## What exactly is a charge-off, and how does it end up on my credit report?

*A question we hear often:* many ITIN holders assume a charge-off means the debt is forgiven. It is not.

A charge-off is a declaration by a creditor that a debt is unlikely to be collected. It happens when a consumer goes severely delinquent, and creditors traditionally make that call at six months without payment. From an accounting standpoint, the lender writes the balance off its books. But the debt stays legally valid. "Written off as uncollectable" is an internal accounting move, not a legal forgiveness.

When that happens, the lender reports it to the three major credit reporting agencies: Experian, TransUnion, and Equifax. It then sits on your credit report for seven years, even if you pay the balance before then. For ITIN holders, the report ties to your ITIN as the identifying number, so it lands on the same credit file you have been building. There is no separate or more lenient process for ITIN holders. Non-SSN consumers have the same dispute and protection rights as U.S. citizens.

## How much will a charge-off drop my ITIN credit score?

*This one comes up a lot:* the score impact depends on where your score sits before the charge-off hits.

Depending on your credit history, a charge-off can cause a drop of as much as 50-150 points. Some scoring models show steeper damage: a clean 750 score can fall 80-110 points, and the entry stays on your report for seven years from the original date of first delinquency under FCRA Section 605(a)(4).

The damage does not begin the day the charge-off is reported, either. Before the charge-off notation appears, you likely had several missed payments already on record for that account. Each of those, at 30, 60, 90, and 120 days late, already reduced your score. This is why ITIN holders going into a charge-off often see a cumulative decline that feels much larger than a single event. Payment history accounts for 35% of your FICO score, and a complete failure to pay is the worst signal that factor can send.

## Is a charge-off different from a collection account on my ITIN report?

*Readers frequently ask* about the distinction because both often appear on a credit report at the same time.

A charge-off is recorded by the original creditor. A collection account is recorded by a separate debt collector that purchased or was assigned the debt after the charge-off. You might end up with both on your report at once: the charge-off from the original creditor and a collection account from whoever bought the debt. One unpaid debt, two separate negative entries, each with its own score impact.

For ITIN holders building their first credit file, this double entry is especially painful because a thin file has fewer positive accounts to offset the damage. If you are in this situation, our guide on [thin credit files with an ITIN](/articles/thin-credit-file-itin-holder) explains how to add positive tradelines alongside negative entries to speed up recovery.

## Does the seven-year clock restart if the debt is sold to a new collector?

No, and this is one of the most important facts to understand.

The seven-year period runs from the original date of first delinquency under FCRA Section 605(a)(4). It does not restart if the debt is sold, transferred, or re-aged. Some collectors try to re-report the debt with a newer date, which would illegally extend its life on your report. If you see a charge-off with a date of first delinquency that looks too recent, that is a legitimate dispute you can file with each bureau. See our full walkthrough on [how to dispute credit report errors with an ITIN](/articles/how-to-dispute-credit-report-errors-with-itin) for the step-by-step process.

## Should I pay a charged-off debt on my ITIN credit report?

This is a nuanced decision with real tradeoffs. Here is a comparison of the two main paths:

| Action | Effect on Credit Report | Effect on Score | Other Considerations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pay in full (no deletion agreement) | Status changes to "paid charge-off" | Little to no immediate score increase | Looks better to future lenders; stops collection calls |
| Negotiate pay-for-delete | Creditor may remove the entry entirely | Potentially large score gain if removed | Creditor is under no legal obligation to agree |
| Settle for less than owed | Status shows "settled"; balance shows $0 | Modest improvement over time | You may receive a 1099-C tax form for forgiven amount |
| Do nothing | Remains "unpaid charge-off" for 7 years | Continued score drag; fades with age | Debt can be sued upon within state statute of limitations |

Paying a charge-off will not remove it from your report, but it changes the status from unpaid to paid. That matters more than most people realize: lenders may require you to clear charge-offs before approving you for new credit. So even without an immediate score jump, paying or settling is usually the right move if you plan to apply for anything soon.

You can dispute charge-off errors with the credit bureaus, request debt validation from collectors, and try negotiating a pay-for-delete agreement. Success rates on pay-for-delete requests are low, but they are worth attempting in writing before you simply pay. Get any deletion promise in writing before you send a payment.

## Can I dispute a charge-off off my ITIN credit report?

*A question we hear often:* ITIN holders sometimes assume they have fewer rights in the credit system. That is incorrect.

If the charge-off is inaccurate in any detail (wrong balance, wrong date of first delinquency, not your account), you have the right to dispute it. You can file disputes directly with the credit bureaus and request debt validation from collectors. Under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, bureaus have 30 days to investigate once you submit a dispute. If the entry cannot be verified, it must be removed.

Common factual errors to check on an ITIN credit file: the date of first delinquency (which controls the seven-year removal date), the balance amount (which should reflect only the original debt, not added fees), and whether the account actually belongs to you rather than someone else. Mixed-file errors are more common among ITIN holders than SSN holders because names and numbers are sometimes matched incorrectly. If you suspect your file contains accounts that belong to someone else, start with our [mixed credit file guide](/articles/mixed-credit-file-itin-holder) before filing a generic dispute.

## How do I rebuild my ITIN credit score after a charge-off?

*Readers frequently ask* whether recovery is even possible. It is, and it begins immediately.

Rebuilding depends on consistent on-time payments, low card balances, and regular credit report monitoring. The most practical approach for ITIN holders is to layer new positive tradelines on top of the negative entry so the charge-off makes up a smaller share of your overall credit picture over time.

Concrete steps:

1. **Add a credit-builder loan.** These products are designed for thin or damaged files and report on-time payments to all three bureaus each month. Visit our [credit builder loans page](/credit-builder-loans) for options that accept an ITIN.
2. **Keep secured card utilization below 10%.** A low utilization rate signals responsible behavior to scoring models. Learn more about [how credit utilization affects your score with an ITIN](/articles/credit-utilization-itin-holders).
3. **Never miss another payment.** Payment history is the single greatest factor in credit scoring. One new 30-day late mark can set back a recovery by months.
4. **Monitor all three bureaus.** Charge-offs sometimes appear differently across Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, and monitoring lets you catch new errors early. Our guide to [credit monitoring with an ITIN](/articles/credit-monitoring-with-itin) covers tools that work with your ITIN number.
5. **Be patient with the timeline.** The older an item gets, the less it drags on your score. A charge-off from three years ago hurts far less than one from last month, even if both are still on the report.

According to FICO data, most consumers who add multiple positive accounts and keep their record clean of new late payments can recover 50-100 points within 12-24 months after a charge-off, even while the entry remains on the file. The score does not wait seven years to improve. It starts recovering as soon as your positive history outweighs the negative.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Does a charge-off affect an ITIN credit score the same way it affects an SSN holder's score?**
Yes. The credit bureaus apply identical scoring rules regardless of whether your identifier is an ITIN or an SSN. A charge-off is treated as a severe delinquency under every major scoring model and will cause a significant score drop either way.

**How long does a charge-off stay on an ITIN credit report?**
A charge-off stays on your credit report for seven years from the date of first delinquency, per the Fair Credit Reporting Act. That seven-year clock does not restart if the debt is sold to a new collector or transferred to a different agency.

**Will paying a charged-off debt remove it from my credit report?**
Not automatically. Paying a charge-off updates the account status from "unpaid" to "paid charge-off," which looks better to future lenders, but the entry itself remains on your report for the full seven years unless the creditor agrees in writing to delete it.

**Can I dispute a charge-off on my ITIN credit report?**
Yes. ITIN holders have the same dispute rights as SSN holders under the Fair Credit Reporting Act. If any detail is inaccurate, such as the date of first delinquency or the balance, you can dispute it directly with each bureau in writing.

**Does a charge-off on my credit report affect my immigration case?**
A charge-off is not directly reported to immigration authorities and does not appear on any USCIS file. However, in some green card or visa contexts, USCIS officers may review a credit report as part of a public charge assessment. A charge-off signals financial difficulty, so resolving it before that review is wise.
