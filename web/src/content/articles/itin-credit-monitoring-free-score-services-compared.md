## Which service actually gives me a FICO score with my ITIN?

*A question we hear often:*

Two services deliver a genuine FICO score to ITIN holders at no cost: myFICO's free plan and Discover Credit Scorecard.

At myFICO, the free plan lets you sign up with an ITIN and provides your Equifax FICO Score 8 along with your complete credit file. Updates arrive monthly, with real-time alerts for hard inquiries or new accounts throughout the month. If your account gets suspended immediately after signup (which is common for ITIN holders), call myFICO support and they'll reactivate it after a few security questions about your credit file. Most people get this resolved in one call under ten minutes.

Discover Credit Scorecard is open to any U.S. resident with a valid ITIN and provides a FICO Score based on your Experian file at no cost after you create a free account. The practical advantage: it runs on Experian data while myFICO's free tier uses Equifax data, so using both together gets you FICO scores from two of the three major bureaus without spending anything. Neither requires you to be an existing customer of those companies.

FICO is used by 90% of top lenders, and while other scores may look similar, they can vary by as much as 100 points from your actual FICO Score. That gap matters when you're preparing to apply for a car loan or rental approval. For deeper context on how the two main scoring models compare for ITIN holders, see our guide on [FICO vs. VantageScore for ITIN holders](/articles/fico-vs-vantagescore-itin-holders).

## What free services show a VantageScore with an ITIN?

VantageScore services are more plentiful and generally easier to sign up for, which makes them a useful starting point for ITIN holders who are new to U.S. credit monitoring.

Credit Karma's U.S. site and app accept ITIN signups and will display your Equifax VantageScore 3.0 with updates every few days. The one signup quirk: it may ask for a U.S. driver's license or state ID. That extra ID step rarely comes up if your credit history is six months or longer. Credit Karma's score is a real, recognized VantageScore 3.0, not a promotional "educational" number. It covers both TransUnion and Equifax at no cost, updating weekly with personalized recommendations.

NerdWallet offers free credit scores and reports, and you can sign up with an ITIN to see your credit score from Experian. The dashboard shows VantageScore 3.0 pulled from TransUnion in most cases, updated weekly, alongside an explanation of the factors moving your score up or down.

Kikoff accepts ITINs in place of Social Security numbers during signup, so anyone with an ITIN can create an account, access their credit information, and start building credit. Once signed up, Kikoff reports your payment activity to the major credit bureaus, which builds positive payment history over time. No hard credit check required to get started.

## Is there a paid service built specifically for ITIN holders?

*This one comes up a lot:*

Yes. MyITINCredit is the clearest example of a service designed from the ground up for ITIN holders rather than retrofitted to accommodate them. It accepts ITINs at enrollment and pulls all three bureaus, providing VantageScore 3.0 and VantageScore 4.0. Reports refresh daily, self-pulls don't lower your score, and the service starts with a $1 trial for 15 days. Identity-theft insurance and bilingual support are included.

For ITIN holders who want all-in-one three-bureau visibility, daily monitoring, and Spanish-language support without stitching together multiple free services, MyITINCredit is the most consolidated option available right now. The tradeoff is the ongoing cost after the trial period ends.

One thing worth keeping in mind: credit monitoring and reporting services for MyITINCredit are provided through an affiliate partnership, and the credit score provided is VantageScore 3.0 or 4.0. Your lender or creditor may use a different scoring model, including different versions of FICO or other proprietary models, when making lending decisions. Monitoring your VantageScore is genuinely useful for tracking trends, but the number your lender sees may differ.

## Does AnnualCreditReport.com work with an ITIN?

*Readers frequently ask:*

This is one of the most important distinctions to get straight before spending time on the wrong approach.

The online portal at AnnualCreditReport.com does not accept ITIN numbers. That site, the only one authorized by the federal government to provide free reports, only accepts Social Security numbers. Enter an ITIN and the system rejects it.

The alternative is a mail request. You can't get your credit score or credit report at AnnualCreditReport.com with an ITIN, but you can request a free credit report from each of the three credit bureaus by mailing the required documents. Each bureau has its own mailing address. Include: your full name, date of birth, current address, ITIN, a copy of a government-issued photo ID, and proof of current address (utility bill or bank statement). Allow two to four weeks for delivery.

One important nuance: the free annual report you receive by mail is a credit report, not a credit score. The free reports you're entitled to under federal law show your credit history but don't include a credit score, which is a separate product. To see your actual score, use one of the digital services in the table above alongside your mail-order reports.

## Why do these services suspend ITIN accounts and how do I fix it?

*A question we hear often:*

Most monitoring services that claim to work for ITIN holders don't actually work smoothly. The ones that do share a similar pattern: automated suspensions, one-call fixes, and odd verification quirks that recycled listicles never warn you about.

The suspension happens for a predictable technical reason. Credit bureau systems were built around the SSN format. When a 9-digit ITIN is entered into a field expecting an SSN, automated fraud-detection logic sometimes flags the account and locks it immediately after creation. This isn't a rejection of your ITIN as a concept. It's a system error triggered by an unfamiliar number pattern.

The most common friction is an automated account suspension immediately after signup, which is resolvable in one phone call or social-media message to the bureau. For myFICO specifically, the support team will reactivate your account after asking a few questions about your credit file, such as the number of open accounts and approximate credit limits. This mirrors what the bureaus do to confirm identity. Have your most recent credit account statements available before you call.

For bureau portals (Equifax and Experian's own websites), the same principle applies: call the consumer support line, explain that you enrolled with an ITIN and were auto-suspended, and provide the verification information they request. Most ITIN holders report this is resolved the same day.

## How do I know which score model a service uses, and does it matter?

FICO is used by 90% of top lenders. VantageScore is common on free monitoring sites. Your scores from each model may vary by 20-40 points.

That gap matters in real situations. If you're tracking your score over time to decide whether to apply for a loan or a rental, the specific number is less important than tracking the trend consistently on the same model. What matters is the direction: are you going up or down, and what's driving the movement.

FICO Score 8 (what myFICO's free plan provides) is the version most commonly used by lenders for general credit decisions. FICO mortgage scores (FICO 2, 4, and 5) are different versions used for home loans and are only available via paid tiers on myFICO or through lender-provided disclosures. For ITIN holders just starting out, FICO Score 8 and VantageScore 3.0 both reliably reflect payment history and utilization trends, which are the two factors that move the needle most. ## Which service should I start with if I'm brand new to U.S. credit?

This is the practical question most readers actually need answered.

It typically takes three to six months of consistent reporting from a lender for the major bureaus to generate a score and a formal file. Until you hit that threshold, no monitoring service will show you a score, because one doesn't exist yet. Your first priority is opening a credit-building account, not downloading a monitoring app.

The fastest on-ramp: open a [credit-builder loan with an ITIN](/articles/credit-builder-loan-with-itin), or a secured card that reports to at least one bureau. After six months of on-time payments, come back to this guide and start with myFICO's free plan. It has the lowest sign-up friction of the FICO services, and its Equifax FICO Score 8 is a real lending-grade score. Layer in Discover Scorecard for Experian coverage, then add Credit Karma for TransUnion visibility.

According to a CFPB report on credit invisibles (June 2025), about 12.5% of U.S. adults don't have a scoreable credit score: 2.7% have no credit record at all and another 9.8% have a record without enough history to generate a score. ITIN holders who are brand new to the U.S. are overrepresented in that second group, which is why building the file comes before monitoring it. Our [how to build credit with an ITIN number](/articles/how-to-build-credit-with-itin-number) guide covers exactly what to open first.

## What documents do I need to sign up for ITIN credit monitoring?

*Readers frequently ask:*

The exact documents depend on the service, but here is what to have ready for every signup attempt:

1. Your ITIN (9-digit number starting with 9, format 9XX-XX-XXXX). Having your original IRS CP-565 ITIN assignment letter nearby speeds up any phone verification call.
2. Government-issued photo ID. A foreign passport, U.S. state ID, or U.S. driver's license. If you do not have an SSN, submitting your request for a free credit report in writing requires you to include one copy of a government-issued identification card and one copy of a current utility bill, bank statement, or insurance statement.
3. Proof of U.S. address. A utility bill, bank statement, or lease agreement in your name, dated within 60 days.
4. U.S. phone number. Most services send a verification code by text. VoIP numbers (Google Voice, Skype) frequently fail this step; a real U.S. mobile SIM works reliably.
5. U.S. email address. Free Gmail, Outlook, or Yahoo addresses all work fine.

If you are asked to submit documents digitally (for Experian's mail-alternative upload process, for example), attach a single PDF combining your ID such as a passport scan, proof of address such as a bank or credit card statement, and your ITIN CP-565 letter with both pages.

## FAQs

Q: Can I check my credit score with an ITIN and no SSN?
Yes. Multiple services accept an ITIN for identity verification, including myFICO's free plan, Experian.com, Credit Karma, NerdWallet, Discover Credit Scorecard, and specialty services like Kikoff and MyITINCredit. You need at least six months of reported credit history before most of these will display a score.

Q: Does AnnualCreditReport.com work with an ITIN?
The online portal at AnnualCreditReport.com does not accept ITINs. The system only processes Social Security numbers. ITIN holders must request free annual reports by mail directly from each bureau, enclosing a copy of their government-issued ID and proof of address.

Q: Will checking my credit score with an ITIN hurt my score?
No. Every monitoring service on this list uses a soft inquiry when you view your own score. When you check your own report through a monitoring service, it is considered a soft inquiry, which has no impact on your credit score.

Q: Does Credit Karma work with an ITIN?
It can work, but results are mixed. Some ITIN holders sign up successfully and receive a VantageScore 3.0 from Equifax updated every few days. Others hit identity-verification friction and are asked for a U.S. driver's license or state ID. This friction is less common once you have at least six months of credit history on file.

Q: What credit score model do free ITIN monitoring services use?
Most free services show a VantageScore 3.0 or 4.0 (Credit Karma, MyITINCredit, NerdWallet). myFICO's free plan shows an Equifax FICO Score 8, the model used by most lenders. Discover Credit Scorecard shows a FICO Score based on Experian data.

Q: Which bureaus does each service cover for ITIN holders?
Experian.com covers Experian only; myFICO free covers Equifax only (paid tiers cover all three); Credit Karma covers Equifax and TransUnion; NerdWallet covers TransUnion primarily; Discover Scorecard covers Experian; MyITINCredit covers all three bureaus through its paid subscription.

Q: What should I do if a monitoring service suspends my ITIN account at signup?
Call the service's support line. Answer a few questions about your credit file (account counts, open dates, approximate limits) and the account is typically reactivated during that one call.

Q: Is there a free three-bureau credit score service for ITIN holders?
No fully free, three-bureau score service confirmed as ITIN-compatible exists as of September 2026. The closest free approach is stacking myFICO (Equifax FICO), Experian.com (Experian), and Credit Karma (TransUnion/Equifax VantageScore).

Q: How long do I need to have credit before these services show a score?
Generally, at least six months of account history reported to a bureau is required before that bureau can generate a score. This is a FICO and VantageScore calculation requirement, not a quirk of the monitoring apps. See [how long it takes to build credit with an ITIN](/articles/how-long-to-build-credit-with-itin) for the full timeline.
