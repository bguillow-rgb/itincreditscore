## Why is it so hard to check my credit score with an ITIN?

*A question we hear often:*

Although the ITIN was created for tax purposes, major credit bureaus do accept it to locate a taxpayer's credit history. The problem is not the bureaus themselves. The bureaus maintain ITIN-linked credit files. The problem is the consumer-facing portals sitting in front of those bureaus. Most use automated identity-verification systems hard-coded to expect a nine-digit number that doesn't start with a 9. AnnualCreditReport.com, the only federally authorized site for free reports, accepts Social Security numbers only.

That single gap has a ripple effect: third-party apps pulling from the same pipeline inherit the same restriction. Checking your credit score with an ITIN is possible, but expect variable access, occasional gaps, and the need to confirm that whichever bureau or service you're using actually supports ITIN-based queries before you rely on the result.

There's also a timing problem. It typically takes three to six months of consistent reporting from a lender before the major bureaus generate a score and a formal file. Even if you use the right service, signing up before you have at least six months of history will often return a "no score available" message rather than an actual number. Wait until after your sixth statement before trying any monitoring app.

## What free services actually work with an ITIN?

The landscape is smaller than most articles suggest. Here is what each confirmed free option gives you.

### myFICO (free plan): the best free FICO score for ITIN holders

You can sign up for a free account on myFICO.com to access your monthly FICO Score 8 based on your Equifax credit report. myFICO also offers two paid plans that let you access your FICO scores from all three credit bureaus every one to three months, depending on the plan. The free plan accepts an ITIN entered in the SSN field during signup. You get your Equifax FICO Score 8 on a monthly basis, plus email alerts when the score changes. This is the strongest free option for ITIN holders because FICO Score 8 is the model used by 90% of top lenders.

The paid plans (roughly $19.95 to $39.95 per month depending on the tier) add TransUnion and Experian FICO scores, mortgage-specific FICO versions, and identity theft insurance. For most ITIN holders still in the credit-building phase, the free single-bureau plan is the right starting point. Upgrade only when you're approaching a major credit application and need to see what all three bureaus show.

### Experian.com: free Experian FICO score online, full report by mail

Experian's consumer portal accepts an ITIN at signup and will display your Experian FICO Score 8 for free, updated monthly. The score is visible directly in your online account. If you have an ITIN rather than an SSN, Experian relies on other identification elements to compile your credit history.

If you need the full detailed report rather than just the score, the process shifts to a mail-based request. You submit your ITIN, government-issued ID, and proof of address through Experian's online upload portal, and Experian will mail the full report within 7-12 days to your provided address, completely free under FCRA rules. The upside is that the upload portal means you don't have to send physical documents through the postal system. The downside is the wait for the physical report.

### Credit Karma: frequent updates with one known quirk

*This one comes up a lot:*

Credit Karma's U.S. site and app accept ITIN signups and will display your Equifax VantageScore 3.0 with updates every few days. The only signup quirk is that it may ask for a U.S. driver license or state ID. That request is the source of much confusion in forums. It doesn't happen to every ITIN user, and it rarely comes up if your credit history is six months or longer.

If you do hit the ID request, a state-issued ID from any state satisfies it. Undocumented community members who hold a state ID or municipal ID in states that issue them regardless of immigration status have successfully completed this step. If you can't provide a U.S. state ID, use myFICO's free plan or Experian.com as your primary option instead.

One important model note: Credit Karma does not provide FICO scores. It provides free credit scores and reports from TransUnion and Equifax using the VantageScore 3.0 model. VantageScore 3.0 is a useful indicator of where you stand, but it's not the score most lenders pull when you apply for credit. Use Credit Karma for trend-monitoring and frequent check-ins; use myFICO for the score that actually matters when you're about to apply for something.

### True Finance app: clean ITIN signup, monthly Equifax VantageScore

The True Finance app at jointrue.com lets you sign up with an ITIN without the friction that trips up other services. It pulls your Equifax VantageScore 3.0 and full credit file with all accounts, limits, and hard inquiries, updating once a month. True Finance is less well-known than Credit Karma but is frequently cited by ITIN holders as the smoothest signup experience, with fewer automated account suspensions during identity verification.

## What about services that claim to work but don't?

*Readers frequently ask:*

This is where most guides mislead ITIN holders. Below is the confirmed list of services that don't support ITIN access, sourced directly from the providers themselves.

### NerdWallet: confirmed SSN-only

NerdWallet's own help center states that because its verification method with TransUnion is tied to an individual's Social Security Number, it is not possible to provide a credit score using an ITIN or other methods at this time. Despite showing up in many "best free credit score" roundups, NerdWallet is not an option for ITIN holders as of mid-2026.

### Capital One CreditWise: SSN required at enrollment

CreditWise requires you to be over the age of 18 with a valid Social Security number that can be matched to a credit profile from the TransUnion credit bureau. This is a hard requirement in the service's enrollment flow. ITIN holders cannot create a CreditWise account. Worth noting: in summer 2025, CreditWise switched from VantageScore 3.0 to FICO 8 scores, making it more useful for SSN holders, but the ITIN restriction remains.

### AnnualCreditReport.com: online access SSN-only

You cannot get your credit score or credit report at AnnualCreditReport.com with an ITIN. You can, however, request a free credit report from each of the three bureaus using an ITIN by mailing the required documents. The mail route is free and legally guaranteed under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, but it takes weeks and delivers a report without a score attached.

## Is there a way to check all three bureau scores online with an ITIN?

For years, the answer was no. Two of the three major bureaus required ITIN-based requests by mail. That changed in April 2026.

MyFreeScoreNow announced it is the first credit monitoring site to provide consumers without Social Security numbers with online access to credit reports and scores from all three major credit bureaus: Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. This platform change, enabled through full compatibility with ITINs, expands access to credit monitoring services and financial tools for millions of consumers who have historically been left out of traditional online credit platforms.

Until that launch, two of the three major credit bureaus required ITIN-based credit information requests to be submitted through the mail, with responses also delivered through the mail, creating delays and limiting access to timely financial information. MyFreeScoreNow now supports ITIN-based users directly on its platform, with immediate online access to credit data, monitoring tools, and educational resources.

MyFreeScoreNow is a paid subscription service with a trial period. It is not a permanent free option like myFICO or Credit Karma. But if you want to see all three bureaus in a single online dashboard without mailing anything, it is currently the only service that makes that possible for ITIN holders.

## How do I request my credit report by mail if I have an ITIN?

*This one comes up a lot:*

The mail route is free, guaranteed by federal law, and available from all three bureaus. It's slower and doesn't give you a numeric score, but it gives you the full underlying credit file, which is what you need when disputing errors or confirming that your accounts are reporting correctly.

### Bureau-by-bureau mail addresses and documents required

| Bureau | Mailing Address | Documents Required |
|---|---|---|
| **Experian** | P.O. Box 9701, Allen, TX 75013 | Government ID, proof of address, ITIN CP-565 letter |
| **Equifax** | Annual Credit Report, P.O. Box 105281, Atlanta, GA 30348-5281 | Government ID, utility bill or bank statement |
| **TransUnion** | Consumer Disclosure Center, P.O. Box 1000, Chester, PA 19016 | Government ID, proof of address |

Under federal law, you are entitled to a free copy of your credit report, and TransUnion also provides this through chat at no cost. Start a chat on the TransUnion support page, confirm your identity, and request the report be mailed to your address. It's faster than postal mail and still free.

For Experian specifically, you can use an online upload portal to submit your ID documents digitally rather than mailing physical copies. Attach a single PDF with your ID (such as a passport scan), proof of address (such as a bank or credit card statement or utility bill), and your ITIN CP-565 letter (both pages). This avoids the security risk of mailing original documents.

## Do these checks hurt my credit score?

No. When you check your own report through a monitoring service, it's a "soft inquiry," which has no impact on your credit score. This applies to every service listed in this guide, free or paid. The only type of inquiry that can temporarily lower your score is a hard pull, which only happens when you apply for new credit (a card, loan, or line of credit). Checking your score as often as daily is both safe and, frankly, a good habit.

## What if the service shows "no score available" even after I sign up?

*Readers frequently ask:*

A "no score available" result is almost always a file-thickness problem, not a service error. If a bureau can't match enough credit activity to your ITIN, you may receive a "thin file" notice instead of a numeric score.

Two things must be true before any scoring model can generate a number. First, you need at least one account that has been open and reporting to that specific bureau for roughly six months. Second, that account must not be more than six months past its last activity date. If either condition fails, the model can't score your file.

The fix is to add a reporting tradeline and wait. The fastest ways to do this are a [credit builder loan](/articles/credit-builder-loan-with-itin) (which reports to all three bureaus from day one) or being [added as an authorized user](/articles/authorized-user-credit-building-itin) on someone else's established account. Once you have six months of positive payment history reporting, go back to myFICO or Experian.com and try again. If you already have accounts open but still see no score, it's worth [checking your credit report directly](/articles/how-to-check-credit-score-with-itin-number) to confirm your accounts are actually being reported under your ITIN and not left unlinked.

In practice, Experian's Free Credit Score service and TransUnion's CreditView will produce a score if your ITIN has enough tradeline activity, whereas Equifax tends to limit score access to paid subscriptions or to businesses that have explicitly opted into ITIN-based reporting. This is why myFICO's free plan (Equifax FICO 8) and Credit Karma's ITIN-compatible flow (also Equifax) are particularly valuable: they access Equifax through a data-sharing agreement that bypasses the direct Equifax portal restriction.

## Can an automated account suspension be fixed after signup?

Yes, and this is a detail most guides skip entirely. Many credit monitoring services use automated identity verification that flags ITIN numbers during enrollment, especially when the account is brand new and the credit file is thin. The automated system may suspend your account immediately after you create it, even though you did nothing wrong.

The most common friction is that automated account suspension right after signup, which is resolvable in one phone call or social-media message to the service. Call customer support, explain that you are an ITIN holder rather than an SSN holder, and ask for a manual identity verification. In most cases the account is restored within 24-48 hours. It's a nuisance, not a permanent barrier.

The services with the highest rate of automated suspensions for ITIN users tend to be those that rely most heavily on SSN-matched identity databases for verification. myFICO and True Finance have been reported by ITIN holders as having lower rates of this problem. Experian.com's online signup also tends to go more smoothly because Experian has its own ITIN-linked data on file from the credit file itself.

## FAQs

**Can I check my credit score with an ITIN number?**
Yes. Several free and paid services accept an ITIN in place of an SSN to display your credit score. The main ones are myFICO, Credit Karma, Experian.com, True Finance, and MyFreeScoreNow. Checking your own score is always a soft pull and never hurts your score.

**Does AnnualCreditReport.com accept an ITIN?**
No. AnnualCreditReport.com only accepts Social Security numbers for online access. ITIN holders must request their free credit reports by mailing each bureau directly or use a third-party platform like MyFreeScoreNow that supports ITIN-based online access.

**Does Credit Karma work with an ITIN?**
In most cases, yes. Credit Karma's U.S. site and app accept ITIN signups and will display an Equifax VantageScore 3.0 with updates every few days. Some users are asked for a U.S. driver license or state ID during signup, which is less common once you have at least six months of credit history.

**Does NerdWallet accept an ITIN to show a credit score?**
No. NerdWallet's own help center states that its verification method is tied to a Social Security number and it cannot provide a credit score using an ITIN at this time.

**Does Capital One CreditWise work with an ITIN?**
No. Capital One's own FAQ states that CreditWise requires a valid Social Security number that can be matched to a TransUnion profile. ITIN holders should use myFICO, Credit Karma, or Experian.com instead.

**What is the fastest way to get a FICO score with an ITIN?**
Sign up for a free account at myFICO.com and enter your ITIN in the SSN field. myFICO displays your Equifax FICO Score 8 for free on a monthly basis. You need at least one account with six or more months of reported history before a score will generate.

**Will checking my credit score with an ITIN hurt my score?**
No. Checking your own credit score through any monitoring service is a soft inquiry and has zero impact on your score. Only hard inquiries from credit applications can temporarily lower your score.

**How many credit accounts do I need before a service can show me a score?**
FICO and VantageScore each require at least one account reporting to the bureau for roughly six months before they generate a scoreable file. If you have fewer than six months of history, most monitoring apps will sign you up but show "no score available."

**Can I get all three bureau scores online with an ITIN?**
As of April 2026, MyFreeScoreNow became the first credit monitoring site to offer online access to all three bureau reports and scores for ITIN holders. Previously, two of the three bureaus required mail-based requests.

**What documents do I need to request a credit report by mail with an ITIN?**
Typically you need your ITIN or IRS CP-565 assignment letter, a government-issued photo ID (passport or state ID), and one proof of address (utility bill, bank statement, or lease agreement). Exact requirements differ slightly between Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion.
