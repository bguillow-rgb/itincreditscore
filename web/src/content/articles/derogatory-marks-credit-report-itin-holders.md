Spotting a derogatory mark on your credit report for the first time can be alarming, especially when you are already building U.S. credit as an ITIN holder. The rules governing these marks apply equally to you, and so do your rights to challenge them.

## What exactly is a derogatory mark, and does it work differently with an ITIN?

Derogatory credit is negative information on your credit report that hurts your score. The term covers several specific record types, all of which signal to lenders that a debt obligation was not met as agreed.

ITIN credit scores work identically to SSN-based FICO and VantageScore models. A derogatory mark on an ITIN-based file is processed, weighted, and reported the same way it would be on any other consumer's file. There is no separate or harsher rulebook for immigrants. The Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) covers all consumers in the United States regardless of citizenship or the type of tax identification number on their accounts.

The practical upshot: if a creditor reports a derogatory item in error, you have the same legal right to dispute it as any SSN holder. If the mark is accurate, you have the same timeline before it ages off automatically, seven years for most items.

## What types of derogatory marks can appear on my ITIN credit report?

*A question we hear often:* readers want to know whether the mark they see is a minor blip or a serious problem. Here is a breakdown of the most common types and their typical reporting windows.

Derogatory marks are negative items caused by missed or delinquent accounts, things like late payments, collections, or bankruptcy. The main types you may encounter are:

| Derogatory Mark | Triggered When... | Stays on Report |
|---|---|---|
| Late payment (30+ days) | Payment is 30 or more days past due | 7 years from delinquency date |
| Collection account | Creditor sells unpaid debt to a collector | 7 years from original delinquency |
| Charge-off | Creditor writes debt off as a loss | 7 years from delinquency date |
| Foreclosure | Mortgage lender repossesses property | 7 years |
| Chapter 7 bankruptcy | Court discharges eligible debts | 10 years from filing date |
| Chapter 13 bankruptcy | Structured repayment plan completed | 7 years from filing date |

Payment history accounts for 35% of your FICO Score, making it the single heaviest factor in the calculation. Because of that weight, late payments and collections tend to hit your score harder than most other negative items.

## How much does a derogatory mark actually lower my score?

*This one comes up a lot:* ITIN holders building credit from scratch worry that one mistake could erase all their progress.

The honest answer depends on where your score sits before the mark appears. A single 30-day late payment has a bigger proportional effect on a higher score, say 720, than on a score already recovering in the 580 range. Severity matters too. Bankruptcies and foreclosures tend to do more damage than a single missed payment.

The damage is not permanent, though, and it fades. Negative marks carry the most weight in the first two years, then gradually lose influence. So even if you cannot get a mark removed today, consistent on-time payments on your active accounts will steadily reduce its drag on your score. According to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau (CFPB), the most effective way to offset derogatory marks is to add positive payment history as quickly as possible. A [credit-builder loan](/credit-builder-loans) or a secured card reported to the bureaus can start generating that positive history within months.

## How do I find out which derogatory marks are on my ITIN credit report?

You need to review your credit reports from all three bureaus: Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion. Some creditors only report to one bureau, so checking all three is the only way to get the full picture.

As an ITIN holder you can request your reports through AnnualCreditReport.com. Since 2023, all three bureaus have offered free weekly report access, not just the annual report most people know about. Once you have them, look for sections labeled "negative accounts," "collections," or "public records." That is where late payments, charge-offs, bankruptcies, and other derogatory marks will appear.

For a full walkthrough of how to pull all three reports using your ITIN, see [How to Get Your Free Credit Report With an ITIN](/articles/how-to-check-credit-score-with-itin-number).

## Can I remove a derogatory mark before the seven years are up?

*Readers frequently ask:* whether paying off a collection or old charge-off will make it disappear from their report.

This is the most common question and the honest answer is: sometimes. Accurate, verifiable derogatory items cannot be legally removed through a standard dispute. There are, however, several legitimate paths worth knowing.

Dispute inaccurate items. If a derogatory mark contains errors, a wrong delinquency date, a wrong balance, or an account that does not belong to you, you have the legal right to file a dispute. The credit bureau has 30 days after you submit your dispute to investigate. If the information cannot be verified, the bureau must remove it from your report. For a step-by-step guide on filing disputes as an ITIN holder, see [How to Dispute Credit Report Errors With an ITIN](/articles/how-to-dispute-credit-report-errors-with-itin).

Goodwill deletion. This is a written request to a creditor asking them to remove a mark voluntarily, and it works best when you have an otherwise solid payment history. You go directly to the original creditor, not the bureau. Success is not guaranteed, but it costs nothing and can sometimes work on isolated late payments. Our guide on [goodwill letters for late payments](/articles/goodwill-letter-late-payment-itin-credit-score) walks through exactly how to write one.

Pay-for-delete. Some collection agencies, not all, will agree to remove a collection from your report in exchange for payment. Get any such agreement in writing before you send a single dollar. Major bureaus discourage this practice, so success rates vary by collector.

Wait for failed verification. Even for accurate items, if the creditor or bureau cannot verify the information within 30 days per FCRA requirements, it must be removed.

## What should I do about collections specifically on my ITIN file?

Collections deserve their own attention because they are one of the most disruptive derogatory marks for ITIN holders still building their history. When an account is sent to a collection agency due to non-payment, it becomes a collection account on your report and can significantly lower your score.

Before making any payment on a collection, take two steps. First, validate the debt in writing: ask the collector to confirm the original creditor, the original delinquency date, and the amount owed. Second, check that delinquency date against the seven-year window. If you are close to the limit, waiting may be simpler than paying. Paying a collection does not restart the seven-year clock (that clock runs from the original delinquency, not the payment date), but it does update the account status to "paid," which is less damaging. For a full action plan, see [Collections on Your ITIN Credit Report](/articles/collections-on-credit-report-itin-holders).

## How do I rebuild my ITIN credit score while derogatory marks are still on my report?

You do not need to wait for marks to age off before making real progress. Rebuilding credit requires making timely payments, reducing card balances, and broadening your credit mix. None of that requires a clean report.

Here is a practical sequence for ITIN holders recovering from derogatory marks:

1. Pull all three reports and confirm the marks are accurate. Dispute anything that is not.
2. Open at least one positive reporting account today. A [credit-builder loan](/articles/credit-builder-loan-with-itin) is a good starting point because it adds an installment tradeline with zero upfront credit risk.
3. Keep utilization below 30% on any revolving accounts. Scoring models weigh current utilization heavily, so this has a near-immediate effect. Learn more at [Credit Utilization With an ITIN](/articles/credit-utilization-itin-holders).
4. Pay every active account on time, every month. One additional 30-day late payment compounds the damage significantly.
5. Monitor your file monthly so you catch new negative items early. See [Credit Monitoring With an ITIN](/articles/credit-monitoring-with-itin) for tools that work without an SSN.

The scoring impact of a derogatory mark does lessen over time. ITIN holders who add consistent positive history often see meaningful score recovery within 12-24 months even when old derogatory marks are still visible on the report.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Do derogatory marks affect an ITIN credit score the same way they affect an SSN credit score?**
Yes. The credit bureaus apply the same scoring models to ITIN-based files as to SSN-based files. A derogatory mark is weighted identically regardless of which tax ID is on the account.

**How long does a derogatory mark stay on an ITIN credit report?**
Most derogatory marks, including late payments, collections, and charge-offs, remain for seven years from the original delinquency date. Chapter 7 bankruptcies stay for ten years. These timelines apply equally to ITIN holders.

**Can I dispute a derogatory mark on my ITIN credit report if it is inaccurate?**
Yes. The Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) gives all consumers, including ITIN holders, the right to dispute inaccurate information. Each bureau must investigate within 30 days and remove the item if it cannot be verified.

**What is a goodwill deletion and does it work for ITIN holders?**
A goodwill deletion is a written request asking a creditor to voluntarily remove an accurate negative mark. ITIN holders can send one just like any other consumer. Success is not guaranteed, but it works best when the late payment was isolated and your overall history is otherwise solid.

**Can I build my score back up while derogatory marks are still on my ITIN credit report?**
Yes. Derogatory marks lose scoring impact over time, especially after the first two years. Adding positive accounts, keeping utilization low, and paying on time consistently will improve your score even before the marks age off completely.
