Can you have a credit score with an ITIN?

Yes. You can build and hold a full U.S. credit score with an ITIN, no Social Security Number required. All three nationwide bureaus, Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion, can open a credit file under an ITIN, and once you have at least one account reporting for six months, FICO and VantageScore will generate a score on that file. A credit score with an ITIN is identical to one built with an SSN: it runs 300 to 850, weighs the same five factors (payment history, amounts owed, length of history, new credit, and credit mix), and is read the same way by lenders. The ITIN is simply the tax ID the bureaus use to match your accounts, it does not create a separate or lesser kind of score. If you already have a file, the next step is checking it, covered below.

Why might your score not show up right away?

FICO requires at least one account open for six months and reported within the last six months to calculate a score. If you've never opened a U.S. credit account, or only opened one recently, the bureau may return "file not found" or "insufficient data to score." This is extremely common for ITIN holders who are new to the U.S. financial system. It doesn't mean you have bad credit; it means your credit history doesn't exist yet. VantageScore can often produce a number on a thinner file than FICO, so a free VantageScore tool may show you a score a few months before a FICO-based one does. The fix either way is opening a reporting account (see our building credit guide) and giving it six months to generate a scoreable file.

What's the difference between your credit report and your credit score?

Your credit report lists every account, balance, payment, and inquiry a bureau has on file for you. Your credit score is the three-digit number calculated from that report. You want both: the report to catch errors, and the score to see where you stand with lenders. The free reports you're entitled to under federal law cover the report, not the score. One exception worth knowing: Experian says that when you order your Experian report by phone or by mail, a credit score is included. Otherwise, pair the report with one of the free score tools listed below. For a full walkthrough of reading and disputing the report itself, see how to get your credit report with an ITIN.

Why doesn't AnnualCreditReport.com work with an ITIN?

AnnualCreditReport.com is the only federally authorized site for free credit reports, run jointly by Experian, Equifax, and TransUnion under the Fair Credit Reporting Act. Its online request form asks for a Social Security number, and Experian's own guidance says an ITIN entered in that field is recognized as an invalid SSN. So the online form is the wrong tool for ITIN holders. A rejection there is a limitation of that one portal's identity check, not a sign that you have no credit file. Your right to a free report from each bureau still applies with an ITIN; you exercise it by going to each bureau directly, online, by phone, by chat, or by mail, as laid out in the next section. Always request all three, not just one. Lenders often check a single bureau, your data can differ across them, and an error on one bureau's file may not appear on another.

Equifax

Equifax is the most direct online route. Create a myEquifax account at my.equifax.com and enter your ITIN in the SSN field during registration. From the dashboard you can enroll in Equifax Core Credit, which is free and gives you your Equifax credit report and a VantageScore 3.0 based on Equifax data. You can see the report the same day you register. If the online route fails, call Equifax at 1-800-685-1111.

Experian

Try a free account at experian.com first, entering your 9-digit ITIN where the form asks for an SSN. It works for some ITIN holders and not others, depending on whether Experian can match your file. If it can't, use Experian's online document upload: enter your ITIN as the Social Security number, choose the option for proof of identity when requesting a credit report, and attach your ID, proof of address, and ITIN assignment letter (CP-565) in a single file. Experian mails the report within about 7 to 12 days. You can also mail a written request to Experian, P.O. Box 9701, Allen, TX 75013, with your full name including middle initial and any suffix (Jr., II, III), your date of birth, your addresses for the past two years, a copy of a government-issued photo ID, and a copy of a current utility bill, bank statement, or insurance statement. Phone: 1-888-397-3742.

TransUnion

TransUnion offers a live-chat route that skips mailing documents. Start a chat on TransUnion's support site, ask a live agent for a copy of your credit report by mail, and enter your 9-digit ITIN in the SSN field of the secure form the agent sends. The report typically arrives within 7 to 10 business days. For a written request, mail it to TransUnion, P.O. Box 2000, Chester, PA 19016, or call 1-800-916-8800.

If a bureau can't match your ITIN automatically, a mailed request with a copy of your ITIN assignment letter and a government photo ID is the reliable fallback. For how ITINs interact with each bureau's file-matching, see our credit bureaus and ITIN guide.

What documents should you have ready?

Gather these before you start so you don't get stuck halfway through a signup or a mailed request:

  • Your 9-digit ITIN
  • Full legal name, including middle initial and any suffix (Jr., II, III)
  • Date of birth
  • Your current address and every address from the past two years
  • A copy of a government-issued photo ID (passport, consular ID, state ID, or driver's license)
  • A copy of a recent utility bill, bank statement, or insurance statement showing your current address
  • Your ITIN assignment letter (CP-565), which Experian's upload asks for

Send copies, never originals. Some online signups send a verification code by text, so keep your phone nearby.

Why does signup get flagged or suspended?

A common pattern with ITIN signups is an automated suspension right after you create an account. Fraud-detection systems at bureaus and score services are built around SSN patterns, and an ITIN can trip them. It's a false positive, not a judgment about your file or your identity. The fix is almost always one contact with a human: call or chat with the service's support team, answer a few questions about your credit file (such as which accounts you have and when you opened them), and the account is reactivated, usually the same day. Have your ITIN letter and a recent account statement handy before you call.

What if the bureau returns "no file found"?

A "no file found" response means the bureau has no record of any financial accounts associated with your ITIN. This is normal for ITIN holders who haven't yet opened any U.S. credit accounts. It is not a rejection. The path forward is straightforward: open one account that accepts ITINs and reports to the bureaus, a secured credit card is the most accessible option, and within six months of on-time payments you'll have a scoreable file. See the full guide to building credit history with an ITIN for specific starter tools.

You may also see a score at one bureau and "no score available" at another. Each bureau keeps its own file, and if your lenders report to only one or two of them, the third may not have enough data yet. Accounts that report to all three bureaus close that gap over time.

Why might your score differ between services?

Two things move the number from one tool to the next. First, the bureau: Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion each hold their own file, so the data behind each score can differ. Second, the model: FICO and VantageScore use the same 300-850 range and the same core factors but weigh them differently, and scores from the two models can differ by 20 to 40 points, sometimes more on a newer, thinner file. Use a VantageScore tool to watch trends and catch sudden drops, and check a FICO Score when you want the number most lenders use. Our explainer on FICO vs. VantageScore for ITIN holders goes deeper.

How to dispute errors on your credit report

ITIN holders are somewhat more likely to experience mixed-file errors, where another person's account appears on your file, because bureau matching algorithms weren't historically optimized for ITINs. If you find an error, you have the right to dispute it under the Fair Credit Reporting Act. Each bureau offers a dispute process online, by phone, and by mail. When you submit a dispute, the bureau must investigate and respond within 30 days. Provide as much documentation as possible: copies of your ITIN letter, identity documents, and any records showing the account isn't yours. If you suspect your file has been mixed with someone else's, list every item that doesn't belong to you, including unfamiliar addresses, in your dispute.

For bureau-specific processes and how ITINs interact with file matching, see our credit bureaus and ITIN guide.

What comes after checking your score?

Once you know your starting point, you can build a clear plan. If you have no file yet, start building one. If you have a score but want it higher, follow the score improvement guide. If you see errors, dispute them first, fixing inaccuracies is the fastest legitimate way to improve a score. Before any major credit application, check all three bureaus a month or two ahead so there's time to fix errors before a lender pulls your file. Common questions

Can I check my credit score with an ITIN instead of an SSN?

Yes. If you have a credit file at any of the three bureaus, you can get your report and a score using your ITIN. You go to each bureau directly rather than through AnnualCreditReport.com: myEquifax online, Experian online or by mail, and TransUnion by live chat, phone, or mail. Free tools such as myFICO's free plan and Equifax Core Credit add an ongoing score.

Does AnnualCreditReport.com work with an ITIN?

Not online. The AnnualCreditReport.com request form asks for a Social Security number, and Experian notes that an ITIN entered there is read as an invalid SSN. A rejection on that site does not mean you have no credit file. Request your free report from each bureau directly instead.

What if the bureau says "no file found"?

"No file found" simply means the bureau has no record of your financial accounts yet, not that you're blocked from having a score. Open a reporting account (secured card or credit-builder loan) and you'll have a file within a few months.

How often can I check my credit for free?

There is no limit on checking your own score, because it is a soft inquiry. Federal law entitles you to a free report from each bureau every year, and that right covers ITIN holders. Because the AnnualCreditReport.com online form needs an SSN, ITIN holders exercise it by contacting each bureau directly.

Will checking my credit score hurt it?

Checking your own credit score is a soft inquiry and does not affect your score at all. Only a hard inquiry, when a lender pulls your credit to make a lending decision, can temporarily lower your score.

How long does it take to get my credit report by mail with an ITIN?

Mailed requests to Experian and TransUnion usually take two to four weeks. Two faster routes: TransUnion's live-chat request, where the report typically arrives in 7 to 10 business days, and Experian's online document upload, where the report is mailed within about 7 to 12 days. myEquifax shows your Equifax report the same day you register.

Why did my account get suspended when I signed up with my ITIN?

Automated fraud filters at bureaus and score services are built around SSN patterns, and an ITIN can trip them. It is a false positive, not a rejection of your file. One call or chat with the service's support team, answering a few questions about your credit file, usually restores access the same day.

What is the free way to see a FICO score with an ITIN?

myFICO's free plan accepts ITIN signups and shows your Equifax FICO Score 8 with monthly updates and alerts for new hard inquiries or accounts. The Bilt Rewards app shows an Experian FICO Score 9. Experian also includes a credit score when you order your Experian report by phone or mail.
