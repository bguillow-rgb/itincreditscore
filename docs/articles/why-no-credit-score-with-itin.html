Why You Have No Credit Score With an ITIN (and How to Fix It)

By Research Desk · Scores & Credit Building · Published August 28, 2026

Advertiser disclosure:ITIN Credit Score is an independent resource. Some links on this page are from partners who compensate us, which may affect which products we feature and where. This does not change our editorial guidance and is never a cost to you. See our full disclosure.

Quick Answer

You have no credit score with your ITIN because you either have no reported accounts, less than six months of account history, or your lender is not reporting activity to a bureau. FICO requires one account at least six months old with recent activity. Fix it by opening one reporting account and paying on time for six months.

No number on the screen. Just a message saying “no score available” or “insufficient credit history.” If you’re an ITIN holder who has been trying to check your credit score and keeps hitting this wall, you’re not alone, and the reason is almost always one of three fixable problems. This guide explains what is happening and what to do next.

Wait, can ITIN holders even have a credit score?

Yes, completely.

ITIN credit scores work identically to SSN-based FICO and VantageScore models. Credit reporting agencies treat the ITIN as a legitimate identifier, so when you open a secured credit card or take out a small loan using your ITIN, that activity gets recorded in your credit file just like it would for an SSN holder. There is no legal barrier, no immigration status requirement, no waiting list. The bureaus simply need data to work with. No data, no score.

According to Experian’s 2026 white paper, the IRS has issued more than 27 million individual taxpayer identification numbers since 1996, yet a meaningful share of those holders still show up as “credit invisible” because the accounts they hold never reach a bureau’s file.

Why does my credit check show “no score available” with my ITIN?

A question we hear often: an ITIN holder opens a bank account, maybe a secured card too, and then tries to check their score only to find nothing there. Here is the short list of causes.

1. No reporting account. Building an ITIN credit score requires opening accounts that report activity to Equifax, TransUnion, or Experian. Without reported account activity, no score is generated. Not every lender reports to every bureau. Some community banks and credit unions report to only one or none. Confirm with your lender in writing that it reports to all three major bureaus before you open an account specifically to build credit.

2. Account is under six months old. For FICO’s scoring models, you need a credit account that is at least six months old and credit activity on that account during the previous six months. If your secured card is four months old, FICO literally cannot score you yet. Keep paying on time and wait.

3. A bureau-specific file mismatch. The three major credit bureaus each have slightly different policies about accepting an ITIN in place of a Social Security number, so some will generate a full credit report and score while others may show a “thin file” or fail to match the identifier at all. You could have a score at Equifax but show “no score” at Experian. Pull all three reports to see where the gap is.

What is the difference between “no score” and “no credit file”?

Readers frequently ask: these two messages look similar on a monitoring app but they mean different things, and the fix is different for each.

A “no credit file” message means the bureau has zero records linked to your ITIN. No accounts have ever been reported under your number at that bureau. This is the starting point for most new ITIN holders, and the fix is to open at least one account with a lender that reports to that specific bureau.

A “no score available” message means the bureau does have a file for you, but it doesn’t yet meet the minimum requirements to generate a number. If you’ve established a modest credit history, you will likely see a numeric score eventually; if not, the bureau may return “no score available” or present a limited view that only reflects the few accounts it can locate.

The practical difference: if you are “no file,” your first task is to get any reporting account open. If you are “no score,” your accounts exist but are either too new, too few, or not meeting the activity requirement. Check your actual credit report (not just the score tab) to confirm which situation applies. Our guide on how to get your free credit report with an ITIN walks through requesting all three bureau reports step by step.

Does FICO or VantageScore score ITIN holders faster?

This one comes up a lot. The two models have different minimum requirements, which matters when you are just starting out.

FICO 8 / FICO 91 account, at least 6 months old1 (with recent activity)Most widely used by lenders
VantageScore 3.0As little as 1 month of history1Scores thin files sooner
VantageScore 4.0As little as 1 month of history1Can include rent and utility data

VantageScore’s models don’t require as much history as FICO to produce an initial score. VantageScore 4.0 is designed specifically for consumers with limited credit histories, in part by allowing rent and utility payments to factor into a credit file. This is why many ITIN holders see a VantageScore on Credit Karma or Equifax’s consumer portal before they ever see a FICO number. Both scores run on the same 300-850 scale, but lenders often rely on FICO for final approval decisions, so reaching FICO’s six-month threshold still matters.

For a deeper comparison of how these models treat ITIN files differently, see our article on FICO vs. VantageScore for ITIN holders.

My lender says they report, so why is nothing showing at the bureau?

There are two layers to check. First, confirm the lender reports at all. Many smaller community banks and fintech firms now do this automatically, but some don’t, and if a creditor fails to submit reports, there is nothing for the bureaus to evaluate. Second, confirm they report to the specific bureau you are checking. A lender might report to Equifax but not Experian, leaving you “no score” at Experian even after a year of good payments.

Ask your lender which of the three bureaus they report to, then verify by pulling each bureau’s report separately. If the account doesn’t appear on a bureau’s report, it is as if the account never existed for that bureau’s scoring model.

If the account does appear on the report but you still see no score, you are almost certainly hitting the six-month FICO requirement. At that stage, the only path forward is patience and one more on-time payment each month.

What is the fastest way to get my first ITIN credit score?

The math is straightforward: you need one account, reported to at least one bureau, that reaches six months of age with on-time payment history. The fastest tools for hitting that mark are a credit builder loan or a secured credit card. Both are designed for thin-file and no-file borrowers, and most report to all three bureaus.

There is no set starting score for first-time credit builders, but the first number you see may land in the “fair” range, a FICO score of 580 to 669. That’s not a bad place to begin. From fair, consistent behavior can move you to “good” (670+) within another 12-18 months.

Two habits that speed things up: pay every statement on time (payment history is the single largest factor in both FICO and VantageScore), and keep the balance on any card you open below 30% of its credit limit. See our full breakdown on credit utilization with an ITIN for exact numbers.

Once you have more than one account open, adding a second type of credit (a credit builder loan if you already have a card, for example) can strengthen your file further. Our article on how many accounts you need to build a credit score with an ITIN covers how many tradelines actually move the needle.

What happens to my score if I get an SSN later?

Many ITIN holders eventually become eligible for a Social Security number and worry that switching identifiers will wipe their score. It doesn’t. After you receive confirmation from the IRS that your records have been merged, contact each of the three credit bureaus in writing. Include a copy of the IRS confirmation letter, your new SSN, and your old ITIN, and ask each bureau to merge your ITIN credit history into a file under your SSN. The transfer typically takes 30 to 60 days and should not affect your credit score, because the underlying account history stays the same.

The ITIN vs. SSN distinction affects your starting point, not your outcome. Strong credit can be built with an ITIN and carried forward when you transition to an SSN. For the complete process, see our dedicated guide on how to transfer your ITIN credit history to an SSN.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can an ITIN holder have a credit score? Yes. The three major bureaus (Equifax, TransUnion, Experian) can create and maintain a credit file under an ITIN. Once you meet minimum scoring requirements, a FICO or VantageScore is generated the same way it would be for an SSN holder.

How long does it take to get a first credit score with an ITIN? FICO requires one account that is at least six months old with credit activity in the past six months. Most ITIN holders see their first score six to seven months after opening their first reporting account.

Does VantageScore generate a score faster than FICO for ITIN holders? Yes. VantageScore can produce a score with as little as one month of history on one account, so you may see a VantageScore before you qualify for a FICO score.

What if I have accounts but still see “no score available”? Your lender may not be reporting to that specific bureau, your account may be under six months old (for FICO), or there may be a file mismatch where the bureau cannot link your account to your ITIN. Pull your credit report first to confirm the account appears there at all.

Will my score reset to zero if I later get an SSN? No. When you notify the credit bureaus and request your credit history be merged under your new SSN, the underlying account history carries over. The transfer typically takes 30-60 days and should not change your score.
