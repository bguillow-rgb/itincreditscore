Checking your credit score with an ITIN is possible in 2026, and the options have improved noticeably this year.

In April 2026, MyFreeScoreNow announced it had become the first credit monitoring site to give consumers without an SSN online access to all three major bureaus. That matters because, until that point, two of the three bureaus required ITIN-based requests to go through the mail, with responses also delivered by mail. Delays were the norm, not the exception.

This guide covers every verified method available to ITIN holders as of August 2026: free apps, bureau-direct portals, paid monitoring services, and the mail or phone fallback. Each entry is sourced from current, verifiable information. Methods are ranked by ease of online access first, then by bureau coverage and cost.

The core problem: why can’t I just use AnnualCreditReport.com?

The online portal AnnualCreditReport.com does not accept ITIN numbers. That site, the only one authorized by the federal government to provide free reports, only accepts Social Security numbers. Enter an ITIN and the system rejects it.

This is the most common frustration ITIN holders run into, and it leads many people to assume they have no credit file. Often they do. The rejection is a technology limitation of that specific portal, not a statement about your credit standing. A failed identity verification there simply means you need a different channel.

Under federal law, everyone in the United States has the right to request a free copy of their credit report each year from each of the three major bureaus. That right covers ITIN holders too. The methods below are all the documented ways to exercise it, plus additional tools that show a numeric score (not just a report) with an ITIN.

Which free apps actually work for ITIN holders?

A question we hear often:

The three most reliable free tools are Capital One CreditWise, NerdWallet, and Equifax Core Credit. All three use soft inquiries, so checking leaves no mark on your file.

Capital One CreditWise is free and accepts ITINs. It updates weekly and includes credit monitoring. After a 2025 update, CreditWise moved from VantageScore 3.0 to FICO Score 8. It tracks changes to your Experian and TransUnion credit reports, including hard inquiries, new accounts, delinquencies, and balance or personal information changes. The score itself, though, is drawn only from your TransUnion file. No Capital One account required.

NerdWallet lets you sign up with an ITIN and see your Experian credit score. The dashboard also includes full credit reports from TransUnion, plus some financial education content beyond the score.

Equifax lets you register at my.equifax.com and enter your ITIN in the SSN field. You get a free VantageScore 3.0 and a complete credit file once a month. This is the only bureau-direct portal currently confirmed to accept ITIN entry in the SSN field without defaulting to a mail request.

What about Experian’s free score service with an ITIN?

This one comes up a lot:

Experian’s consumer-direct portal (experian.com) works for some ITIN holders but not all. The three major bureaus can each generate a credit file for an ITIN holder, but they don’t always display a traditional credit score alongside it. Some lenders and fintech platforms that partner with a specific bureau can pull a score linked to your ITIN, while the bureau’s own consumer portals may only surface the underlying report.

In practice, a more reliable route to an Experian FICO score with an ITIN is through the Bilt Rewards app. Bilt lets you create an account with an ITIN and provides your Experian FICO Score 9 monthly. Another confirmed Experian score source is Discover Credit Scorecard, which is free even if you’re not a Discover customer. It shows your score and breaks down the factors affecting it; you’ll need an ITIN to set up the account.

Experian’s Free Credit Score service and TransUnion’s CreditView will produce a score if your ITIN has enough tradeline activity. Equifax tends to limit score access to paid subscriptions or to businesses that have opted into ITIN-based reporting. Because this landscape is still shifting, score availability can vary from one bureau to another, and even from one month to the next.

The practical takeaway: check two or three services across different bureaus rather than relying on a single one. Discrepancies are normal, especially early in your credit-building process.

What is the new MyFreeScoreNow ITIN service, and is it worth it?

Readers frequently ask:

In April 2026, MyFreeScoreNow announced it had become the first credit monitoring site to give consumers without Social Security numbers online access to credit reports and scores from all three major bureaus: Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. It’s the first service to offer true three-bureau online access built specifically for ITIN holders, replacing the need to send separate mail requests to multiple addresses.

Until that announcement, two of the three bureaus required ITIN-based requests to go through the mail. MyFreeScoreNow now handles ITIN-based users directly on its platform, with immediate online access to credit data, monitoring tools, and educational content.

The service costs a monthly fee after a trial period, so it makes the most sense for ITIN holders who want three-bureau tracking in one dashboard and find the piecemeal approach of three separate free apps too cumbersome. A competing paid option is MyITINCredit.com, which provides VantageScore 3.0 and VantageScore 4.0 and offers full three-bureau credit scores and reports with daily monitoring alerts. It starts at $1 for 15 days, then $24.95 per month.

How do I get my credit report by mail or phone if the online options fail?

When online sign-up verification fails, mail and phone are your guaranteed fallback. All three bureaus accept ITIN requests by phone or mail, and the documents you need are largely the same across all three.

What to include in every mail request:

  • A cover letter with your full legal name, ITIN, date of birth, and current address
  • A copy of your government-issued photo ID (passport, consular ID, state ID, or driver’s license)
  • A copy of a document showing your current address (utility bill, bank statement, or lease)

Send copies, never originals. Use certified or tracked mail so you have proof of delivery.

What if I get a “no score available” message instead of a number?

A question we hear often:

If a bureau can’t match enough credit activity to your ITIN, you’ll get a “thin file” notice instead of a numeric score. This isn’t an error. It means the scoring model doesn’t have enough data yet to calculate a number.

For example, an ITIN holder with only a secured credit card that opened six months ago may see a “no score available” notice from a bureau that requires at least six months of activity and two separate accounts. Classic FICO Score models generally need at least one account open for six months and one account reported within the past six months before a score can be generated.

If you get that message, the issue is a thin file, not a broken process. The fix is to add one or two more reporting accounts. A credit-builder loan is the most accessible option because approval doesn’t depend on an existing score and payments report to all three bureaus. Becoming an authorized user on a family member’s account can add history to your file immediately without requiring a new credit application.

Once your file has at least two accounts with six or more months of reporting history, most scoring models will produce a number. For a full walkthrough on building from zero, see our guide on how to build credit with an ITIN number.

Free vs. paid: which method is right for you?

Readers frequently ask:

The free tools in this guide cover most ITIN holders’ everyday needs. For most people starting out, a two-app combination covers all three bureaus at no cost: use Capital One CreditWise for your TransUnion FICO Score 8 and Bilt or Discover Credit Scorecard for your Experian FICO Score 9 or 8. For Equifax, register at my.equifax.com and opt into Core Credit for a free monthly VantageScore 3.0.

The main drawback of the free approach is that your scores come from three separate apps with separate logins and no unified monitoring dashboard. Score availability can also vary from one bureau to another and from one month to the next as the reporting landscape continues to shift.

Paid services (MyFreeScoreNow, MyITINCredit.com, Experian IdentityWorks) consolidate all three bureaus under one login and typically add daily monitoring alerts and identity theft protection. Experian IdentityWorks includes reports from all three bureaus plus dark web scans, reports a FICO score rather than a VantageScore, and offers identity insurance up to $1 million for select plan holders. After the 30-day free trial, the cost runs between $9.99 and $19.99 per month.

Pay for a monitoring service if you’ve already built a substantial credit file and need daily alerts, if you’re preparing to apply for a mortgage or auto loan and want three-bureau data in one view, or if you’ve previously been a victim of identity theft and need ongoing fraud monitoring.

Stick with free tools if you’re in the early stages of building credit, have a thin file, or simply want to confirm your accounts are reporting correctly.

Does my ITIN score work the same way as an SSN-based score?

The underlying math is identical. When you use an ITIN for credit products, lenders report your activity to the bureaus using that ITIN as your identifier. Over time, the bureaus build a credit file associated with your ITIN containing your payment history, credit accounts, and other financial information. Your score is calculated from that file exactly as it would be with an SSN.

The scoring model is the same. Your ITIN is simply the key the bureau uses to locate your file, not a separate scoring category.

One practical difference: some lenders and landlords pull credit through portals that aren’t ITIN-compatible and may return a blank report even if your file is fully populated. This is a lender technology limitation, not a file problem. The fix is to ask the lender to submit a manual request using your ITIN. For more on how scoring mechanics apply to your situation, see our full guide at how credit scoring works with an ITIN.

The scale of the population dealing with this is also worth noting. More than 31 million ITINs have been issued since their introduction, with approximately 5 million currently active as of March 2026. In calendar year 2025 alone, 469,888 ITINs were issued. The tooling for ITIN holders has improved meaningfully in the past 12 months as a direct result.

For a reader starting completely from scratch, here is the fastest reliable path to seeing a number today.

Step 3: Add Equifax via myEquifax. Go to my.equifax.com and create an account, entering your ITIN in the SSN field. Opt into Core Credit. Equifax now includes an Optimal Path credit score planner inside the myEquifax mobile app, which uses your current Equifax credit profile to suggest specific tasks for the next 30 days toward a target VantageScore 3.0 goal.

Step 4: Add an Experian source. Download the Bilt Rewards app or register for Discover Credit Scorecard to see your Experian FICO Score. This gives you three-bureau coverage at zero cost.

Step 5: Request a full report by mail if online verification fails. If any of the portals above can’t verify your identity, fall back to the mail or phone method using the addresses in the table above. You’ll receive the full report within 2-4 weeks. That report gives you the underlying data; pair it with the score you pulled from a working free tool.

FAQs

Can I check my credit score online with an ITIN instead of an SSN?

Yes. Several platforms accept an ITIN for online score access, including Capital One CreditWise, NerdWallet (Experian score), Equifax Core Credit at my.equifax.com, Bilt Rewards (Experian FICO 9), and MyFreeScoreNow, which became the first service to offer all-three-bureau online access for ITIN holders in April 2026.

Does AnnualCreditReport.com work with an ITIN?

No. The online request system at AnnualCreditReport.com does not accept ITINs, so you’ll need to request your credit reports by mail or access your score through a bank or credit card account that already has your ITIN on file.

Does checking my own credit score hurt my ITIN credit score?

No. All methods described here use a soft inquiry. Checking your own credit does not affect your score.

What score model do ITIN-friendly tools show?

It varies by service. Capital One CreditWise shows FICO Score 8 from TransUnion. NerdWallet shows a VantageScore from TransUnion. Equifax Core Credit shows VantageScore 3.0 from Equifax. Bilt shows Experian FICO Score 9. For a full explanation of why your score differs between services, see our guide on why your credit score differs by bureau with an ITIN.

What documents do I need to request my credit report by mail with an ITIN?

You typically need a copy of your government-issued photo ID, a document showing your current address (utility bill or bank statement), and a cover letter with your full legal name, ITIN, date of birth, and current address. Send copies, never originals.

How long does a mail request for a bureau credit report take?

Expect 2-4 weeks for Equifax and Experian. The TransUnion live-chat method is faster, with the physical report typically arriving within 7-10 business days.

Why does my ITIN show a score on one bureau but not another?

Each bureau maintains its own file independently. If a lender only reports to one or two bureaus, the third may have too little data to generate a score. This is common, especially early on.

Do I need a credit history before I can check my ITIN credit score?

You need at least six months of history on at least one account before most scoring models can generate a number. Before that threshold, most services will return a no-score message.

Is it safe to enter my ITIN on a credit monitoring website?

Stick to well-known platforms and bureau-direct portals. Avoid any site that charges a large upfront fee just to view your report. The services listed in this article use standard encryption and are established U.S. businesses or bureau-direct properties.

Can I use Credit Karma with an ITIN?

Generally no. Credit Karma relies on the Social Security number for identity verification and credit report access. Capital One CreditWise, NerdWallet, and Equifax Core Credit are the most reliable free alternatives.

Why does this matter? The scale of the problem

According to a June 2025 CFPB report, roughly 25 million unscored individuals represent nearly 10% of the adult population in the U.S. ITIN holders are disproportionately represented in that group, not because they lack financial activity, but because the tools to view and monitor their files have historically required an SSN. Consumers using ITINs contribute significantly to the U.S. economy, with estimated tax contributions exceeding $100 billion annually at the federal, state, and local levels.

The tooling gap is narrowing. MyFreeScoreNow’s ITIN launch in April 2026, Equifax’s Core Credit portal accepting ITINs in the SSN field, and the availability of Bilt and CreditWise mean that a determined ITIN holder can now put together free three-bureau score coverage in under an hour without mailing a single document. The next step after you can see your score is knowing how to move it.
