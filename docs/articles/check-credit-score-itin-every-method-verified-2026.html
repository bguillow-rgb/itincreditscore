Check Your Credit Score With an ITIN: Every Method (2026)

By Editorial Team · Credit Scores & Building · Published September 28, 2026

Advertiser disclosure:ITIN Credit Score is an independent resource. Some links on this page are from partners who compensate us, which may affect which products we feature and where. This does not change our editorial guidance and is never a cost to you. See our full disclosure.

Quick Answer

ITIN holders can check their credit score for free using services including Experian.com, the myEquifax Core Credit portal, myFICO's free plan, and Capital One CreditWise. Each is a soft pull that never harms your score. NerdWallet and AnnualCreditReport.com require an SSN for the online score, though bureaus accept ITIN by mail for reports.

If you have an ITIN instead of a Social Security number, the U.S. credit system will make you feel like you’re hitting a wall at every turn. The good news: checking your credit score with an ITIN is entirely possible, and there are more options than most people know about.

The harder truth is that several widely recommended services, including NerdWallet and the AnnualCreditReport.com online portal, quietly reject ITINs at the identity verification step. Most guides recycle the same incomplete list without telling you which services actually work and which ones waste your time.

This article is different. We verified each method against current service documentation and user reports as of September 2026. The table below gives you the full picture at a glance, and every section below it explains exactly what to do, what friction to expect, and how to fix it.

Do ITIN holders actually have a credit file to check?

All three major bureaus, Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, can generate a credit file for an ITIN holder. Whether a score exists depends entirely on what’s in that file.

If a bureau can’t match enough credit activity to your ITIN, you may get a “thin file” notice instead of a number. Once you start establishing regular payments, like rent, utilities, or credit-builder loans, each bureau is more likely to generate a usable score, though the exact algorithm and timing differ across the three agencies.

The practical threshold: most scoring models, both FICO and VantageScore, require at least one account with six or more months of reported payment history before a score can be calculated. If you haven’t opened a reporting account yet, no monitoring service will show you a number. For guidance on building that first account, see our guide to credit builder loans with an ITIN or how to build credit with an ITIN number.

According to the CFPB’s June 2025 report on credit invisibles, about 12.5% of U.S. adults either have no credit record at all (2.7%) or have a record without a scoreable file (9.8%). ITIN holders are disproportionately represented in that group, simply because fewer lenders historically accepted ITINs for reporting. That gap is closing, but it means the first step for many ITIN holders isn’t “check my score” but “build my file.” Both steps are covered here.

The accounts-suspended problem: why does this keep happening to ITIN holders?

A question we hear often: why does every service flag my account immediately after signup?

Services that accept ITINs share a consistent pattern: automated suspensions, one-call fixes, and odd verification quirks. Every method that works goes through some version of this gauntlet. What you actually get at the end, VantageScore 3.0, FICO 8, FICO 9, a full credit file, alerts, depends on small details that determine whether you’ve wasted a week on something that doesn’t work.

The underlying cause is structural. Automated fraud-detection systems at bureaus and third-party monitoring tools are trained on SSN patterns. ITINs begin with the digit 9 and follow a different structure, which trips a flag. This is not a rejection of your file or your identity. It’s a false positive from a system that wasn’t designed with ITIN holders in mind.

The fix is almost always a single phone call. Reach a human agent, answer a few knowledge-based questions about your credit file (account counts, approximate limits, open dates), and the suspension gets lifted. This typically takes fewer than 10 minutes. At myFICO specifically, if your account is suspended right after signup (which is common), one call to their support line resolves it after a few security questions about your credit file. One call, under 10 minutes.

Method 1: Experian.com (free, FICO Score 8, online)

Experian is the most ITIN-friendly of the three major bureaus for online access. With a free Experian account, you get your Experian credit report and your FICO Score, updated daily.

The score is a FICO Score 8 based on Experian data, the same model most lenders use for credit card decisions. FICO Scores are used by 90% of top lenders, so this is as close to a real-world lender view as you can get for free.

How to enroll with an ITIN:

  1. Go to Experian.com and select “Get your free credit score.”
  2. Complete the signup form. Where it asks for your SSN/ITIN, enter your 9-digit ITIN.
  3. Experian will attempt to match your ITIN to your credit file. If you have an ITIN rather than an SSN, Experian will rely on other identification elements to compile your credit history.
  4. If online verification fails, Experian’s mail-based verification is available. Their online upload form lets you submit documentation, with your report mailed in 7-12 days.
  5. For phone assistance, call Experian at (888) 397-3742.

What you get: FICO Score 8 (Experian data), full Experian credit report, daily updates, and score factor explanations. Cost: free, no credit card required.

Method 2: myEquifax Core Credit (free, VantageScore 3.0, online)

This one comes up a lot: readers ask whether Equifax’s free portal actually works without an SSN.

It does. Equifax Core Credit is free and includes a monthly credit score and Equifax credit report. The score is a VantageScore 3.0 based on Equifax data. From your myEquifax account, you can click “Get my free credit score” to enroll and get free daily access to both your Equifax credit report and your VantageScore.

The VantageScore 3.0 runs on the same 300-850 range as FICO but is calculated differently. It’s useful for tracking trends and catching sudden drops, even if the number will differ somewhat from what a lender sees. For a deeper comparison, see our article on FICO Score vs. VantageScore for ITIN holders.

Equifax is a strong choice for ITIN holders, with online registration at my.equifax.com. An automated account flag is possible here too. The resolution is the same: call Equifax directly, confirm your identity. Have your ITIN document and a recent account statement ready before you dial.

One more thing worth knowing: in connection with various settlements, Equifax is making at least six additional free Equifax credit reports per year available online to U.S. consumers on AnnualCreditReport.com through December 31, 2026. That’s a report, not a score, but it’s valuable for checking for errors.

Method 3: myFICO free plan (free, FICO Score 8, online)

Readers frequently ask: is there a free way to get an actual FICO score, not just a VantageScore, with an ITIN?

Yes. myFICO’s free plan delivers a FICO Score 8 based on Equifax data, with the subscription renewing monthly at $0.

ITIN holders can sign up for the free plan online. You get your Equifax FICO Score 8, your complete credit file, monthly score updates, and real-time alerts for hard inquiries or new accounts.

This is the most lender-relevant free option available to ITIN holders. Monthly updates are less frequent than Experian’s daily refresh, but the real-time hard-inquiry alerts close that gap for practical monitoring.

For those who want more, myFICO’s paid plans run $19.95 per month for Basic, $29.95 for Advanced, or $39.95 for Premier. Paid plans let you compare FICO Scores and credit reports from all three bureaus side by side. That three-bureau view is particularly useful for ITIN holders since your data may be distributed unevenly across Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion.

Method 4: Credit Karma (free, VantageScore 3.0, online)

Credit Karma is one of the most frequently cited tools in ITIN communities online because it covers two bureaus from a single free account.

Credit Karma’s U.S. site and app accept ITIN signups and display your Equifax VantageScore 3.0 with updates every few days. The only signup quirk: it may ask for a U.S. driver’s license or state ID. This rarely happens if your credit history is six months or longer.

To be clear about what you actually get: Credit Karma provides daily TransUnion and Equifax full reports with VantageScore 3.0. Two bureaus, one free account, frequent updates. That’s genuinely useful for tracking your credit trajectory over time.

The most common friction is an automated account suspension right after signup. For Credit Karma specifically, reaching out via their support chat or social media tends to be faster than the phone queue.

One firm caveat: FICO is used by 90% of top lenders, while VantageScore is common on free monitoring sites. Your scores from each model may vary by 20-40 points. Use Credit Karma to monitor trends and catch sudden changes, but use myFICO or Experian.com when you want the FICO number a lender will actually see.

Method 5: Direct bureau mail request (free, report only, all three bureaus)

A question we hear often: what if all the online methods fail to verify my identity?

The mail request is your guaranteed fallback. Every person in the United States, regardless of whether they have an SSN or an ITIN, has the right to request their credit report from all three major bureaus. That right extends to ITIN holders. Under federal law, everyone is entitled to a free copy of their credit report each year from each bureau.

You cannot get your credit score or credit report at AnnualCreditReport.com with an ITIN via the online portal. You can, however, request a free credit report from each bureau by mailing the required documents.

What to include in a mail request:

  • Full legal name and current address
  • Date of birth
  • ITIN (written clearly)
  • Government-issued photo ID (copy of passport, driver’s license, or consular ID)
  • One proof of address (utility bill, lease, or bank statement)

Your credit report will arrive by mail within 15 days. You’ll receive a detailed report, not a numeric score. But the report is the foundation of everything: it shows which accounts are being reported, lets you check for errors, and helps you prepare for disputes. For a full walkthrough of reading your report, see our ITIN credit report bureau-by-bureau guide.

How do FICO scores and VantageScores differ for ITIN holders?

This one comes up a lot: readers are confused when their Credit Karma VantageScore looks different from the FICO score their lender pulls.

Both FICO and VantageScore use the same 300-850 range and the same five core factors: payment history, amounts owed, length of credit history, new credit, and credit mix. The weighting differs, and the two models treat thin files differently. Your scores from each model may vary by 20-40 points, and for ITIN holders with newer or thinner files, that gap can be even wider. VantageScore can generate a score with as little as one month of history; FICO requires six months.

What if I check my score and get “no score available”?

Readers frequently ask: I signed up for a service and it says I have a file but no score. What does that mean?

If you’ve established some credit history, you’ll likely see a numeric score. If you haven’t, the bureau may return a “no score available” message or a limited view reflecting only the few accounts it can locate.

“No score” is not a rejection. It’s not a problem with your ITIN. It means your file doesn’t yet meet the minimum threshold for the scoring model in use. For FICO Score 8, that minimum is one account open for at least six months and reported within the last six months. For VantageScore 3.0, the bar is lower, usually one to two months of history.

The path forward: open one account that reports to the bureau where you got “no score,” keep it in good standing, and wait six months. A credit builder loan with an ITIN is one of the most reliable ways to build that history deliberately. For a full discussion of thin files, see our guide on thin credit file with an ITIN.

How often should I check my ITIN credit score?

There’s no limit to how often you check your own score. All monitoring methods are soft pulls, which don’t affect your credit score. Only hard pulls, made when you apply for credit, can lower your score temporarily.

A practical monitoring routine for ITIN holders:

  • Monthly: review your FICO Score 8 from myFICO’s free plan or Experian.com and note the top score factors.
  • Every few days (or set up alerts): Credit Karma’s live alerts cover Equifax and TransUnion VantageScores and flag sudden changes quickly.
  • Quarterly: pull a full credit report from one bureau in rotation. With three bureaus and Equifax’s current settlement offering up to seven free reports per year through 2026, you can maintain regular report access at no cost.
  • Before any major credit application: check all three bureaus at least 30-60 days in advance so you have time to dispute errors before a lender pulls your file.

Understanding why your score might show different numbers at different services is covered in our guide on why your credit score differs by bureau with an ITIN.

Call Equifax at (888) 548-7846 if your account gets flagged.

This gives you FICO Score 8 on Equifax data, monthly updates, and real-time hard-inquiry alerts. If your account is suspended right after signup, call myFICO support. They’ll reactivate it after a few security questions about your credit file, account counts, limits, open dates. One call, done.

Step 4 (optional): Credit Karma (Equifax + TransUnion VantageScore 3.0, free) Credit Karma’s U.S. site and app accept ITIN signups and display your Equifax VantageScore 3.0 with updates every few days. This step is optional since Equifax is already covered by Step 2, but the TransUnion VantageScore and frequent update cadence add useful monitoring coverage.

Once all three accounts are active, you have daily visibility across all three bureaus from your phone, at zero cost, with no SSN required.
