ITIN Credit Monitoring: Every Service Verified (2026)

Six services accept an ITIN for online credit monitoring in 2026: Equifax Core Credit (free, VantageScore 3.0), myFICO Free (free, FICO 8), MyFreeScoreNow (paid, all 3 bureaus), Credit Karma (free, Equifax only), MyITINCredit (paid, all 3 bureaus), and myEquifax Premier (paid, all 3 bureaus). Credit Karma, Credit Sesame, and AnnualCreditReport.com do not fully support ITINs.

Why do so many services block ITIN holders at signup?

The root issue is identity verification. Most mass-market credit monitoring apps use automated knowledge-based authentication (KBA), a process that cross-references your SSN with public records to confirm your identity in real time. AnnualCreditReport.com does not work online with an ITIN because it requires an SSN; ITIN holders must request reports by phone or by mailing the Annual Credit Report Request Form.

The same automated bottleneck hits nearly every major third-party monitoring app. ITIN holders have historically faced significant barriers to accessing their credit reports and scores online, leaving many limited in their ability to understand, build, and manage their credit, which in turn restricts access to loans, housing, and competitive financial products.

That does not mean your credit file does not exist. The credit bureaus will create a file for you using your ITIN if that is your identifying number. The challenge is purely at the monitoring-service level, and a handful of platforms have solved it. The ones below have been confirmed to accept ITINs as of the verification date above.

What free service gives me an actual score, not just a report?

A question we hear often:

Two free services stand out here, and they serve different needs.

Equifax Core Credit is the most broadly accessible free option. Equifax is the top choice for ITIN holders. You register at my.equifax.com, enter your ITIN in the SSN field, and get a free VantageScore 3.0 and complete credit file. The score updates daily. No credit card required. One known friction point: some ITIN holders see an account suspension error immediately after signup, triggered by the automated verification step. The fix is a short call to Equifax’s customer service at 1-888-378-4329 to reactivate manually.

myFICO Free Plan is the better pick if you want a lender-grade score rather than a VantageScore. ITIN holders can sign up and get their Equifax FICO Score 8 and complete credit file, with monthly updates and real-time alerts for hard inquiries or new accounts. No credit card required. FICO Score 8 is the version most lenders use for everyday credit decisions, so that is a meaningful advantage over a VantageScore alone.

The practical limitation of both free options: they cover only Equifax. If a lender pulls your Experian or TransUnion file, you are flying blind on those bureaus unless you add a paid service or request reports by mail.

What is the best paid option for all-three-bureau monitoring with an ITIN?

This one comes up a lot:

If you want a dashboard watching Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion simultaneously under your ITIN, you have three realistic choices.

MyFreeScoreNow made headlines in April 2026. The company announced it is the first credit monitoring site to give consumers without Social Security numbers online access to credit reports and scores from all three major bureaus: Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion, made possible through full ITIN compatibility. The platform also offers AI-powered credit improvement recommendations through its Optimal Path feature, along with daily monitoring alerts. Pricing runs $19.95-$29.95 per month after a 7-day trial, depending on the enrollment link. The platform lists “ITIN and SSN Compatible” explicitly in its feature set.

MyITINCredit (myitincredit.com) was built specifically for the ITIN population. It helps individuals monitor their credit scores and reports using either an ITIN or an SSN, with monitoring alerts and tools to track changes in their credit profile. The platform monitors all three bureaus daily on a single ITIN dashboard.

myEquifax Premier ($19.95/month) adds Experian and TransUnion data on top of Equifax. The plan covers all three bureaus with monthly refreshes and delivers real-time alerts for new accounts or hard inquiries.

Does Credit Karma actually work with an ITIN?

The honest answer is: partially, and unreliably.

Credit Karma officially requires an SSN to verify your identity, so there is no clean yes or no. Some ITIN holders with 6 or more months of credit history have successfully created accounts by entering their ITIN in the SSN field, triggering fewer automated verification questions.

When it does work, the Credit Karma app can display your Equifax VantageScore 3.0 and credit file with updates every few days, plus live alerts for inquiries, new accounts, or balance shifts. The TransUnion side, though, does not populate for ITIN holders at all.

Do not count on Credit Karma as your primary ITIN monitoring tool. Use it as a supplemental alert layer if it happens to work for you, and rely on Equifax Core Credit or myFICO as your main source of truth.

How do I get my TransUnion and Experian credit reports with an ITIN if I do not want to pay?

Readers frequently ask:

Both Experian and TransUnion provide free reports to ITIN holders under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, but neither offers a free online score dashboard the way Equifax does.

Experian: Use the upload form at experian.com/consumer/upload. Enter your ITIN in the Social Security Number field, select “Proof of Identity - Requesting Credit Report,” then attach a single PDF with your ID (a passport scan works), proof of address (a bank statement or utility bill), and your ITIN CP-565 letter (both pages). If everything is in order, Experian mails the full report within 7-12 days. You get the credit file, not a score.

TransUnion: While many automated systems struggle with ITIN holders, TransUnion offers a live chat method where an agent sends you a secure PII (Personally Identifiable Information) form to submit your ITIN and personal details. Your report is typically mailed out immediately and arrives in a TransUnion envelope within 7-10 business days.

Neither report includes a score number. Federal law does not require bureaus to disclose your score as part of the free statutory report. To see a score from Experian or TransUnion data, you need a paid plan (myFICO Advanced at $29.95/month, myEquifax Premier at $19.95/month, or MyFreeScoreNow) or a financial institution that shows you the score tied to your account.

Why does it matter which bureau my monitoring service uses?

This is not an academic question. Your score at Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion can differ by 20-50 points depending on which lenders report to which bureaus and how each bureau has verified your identity files.

ITIN holders across the country are paying bills, building businesses, and managing their finances, yet many remain underrepresented within traditional lending models. That underrepresentation is partly a data-coverage problem: a lender who reports only to TransUnion will not appear in your Equifax file at all, which can make your Equifax score lower than your TransUnion score.

The stat that shows how much this matters: after 12 months, 76.9% of ITIN holders remained current on trades, a rate 15% higher than SSN consumers, according to Experian’s February 2026 white paper by Theresa Nguyen. ITIN holders are, on aggregate, strong credit performers. The gap between your score at one bureau versus another is usually a data-coverage issue, not a behavioral one, and that is fixable by making sure your credit-building accounts report to all three bureaus. See our guide to building credit with an ITIN number for a deeper look at which lenders and products report to all three.

What score will I actually see, and is it the same score a lender uses?

This one comes up a lot:

Most free monitoring services deliver a VantageScore 3.0, which is a real score built from the same underlying data as FICO but calculated with a different formula. Both models use the 300-850 range and the scores generally track closely, but they are not identical.

The key distinction: the vast majority of U.S. lenders use a FICO Score (most commonly FICO 8) for credit decisions, not VantageScore. If you want to know the number a lender is likely to pull, the myFICO Free Plan’s Equifax FICO Score 8 is more directly comparable. Each myFICO subscription includes at least a standard FICO 8 score, and the paid plans add scores used for mortgages, auto loans, and credit card decisions.

For day-to-day credit building and monitoring, a VantageScore from Equifax Core Credit is perfectly adequate to track your progress and catch errors. Before a loan application, though, it is worth checking your actual FICO Score on myFICO’s free plan first so there are no surprises.

To understand how both scoring models treat ITIN holders in detail, see our comparison guide on FICO Score vs. VantageScore for ITIN holders.

Are there any ITIN-specific credit monitoring tools worth knowing about?

A question we hear often:

Beyond the mainstream services above, two platforms are built specifically for the non-SSN population.

Creditship is described as a free AI-powered credit monitor that tracks all three bureaus for ITIN holders and provides concrete steps to lift your score, positioning itself as a simple way to see your number without an SSN and understand which actions move it most. Availability and exact pricing were not independently confirmed at time of writing; verify directly before relying on it.

MyITINCredit is the most established ITIN-specific monitoring platform. In addition to credit scores and reports, it provides monitoring alerts for changes on your credit file, early detection of potential fraud or identity theft, and tools to track factors affecting your score, built for both ITIN and SSN users across the United States.

Neither of these replaces a dispute or fraud-recovery process. If you find errors on your report, see our step-by-step guide on how to dispute credit report errors with an ITIN.

What if my monitoring service suspends my account after signup?

Account suspensions immediately after ITIN-based signup are extremely common and do not mean you have done anything wrong. They are typically triggered because the automated system does not recognize an ITIN the same way it recognizes an SSN.

The fix is nearly always the same: call the customer service number for the bureau or service, explain that you used an ITIN rather than an SSN, and ask for manual verification. Resolution typically takes one phone call of 10-20 minutes. Equifax’s number is 1-888-378-4329. For myFICO, use the support link inside your account dashboard.

A few practical tips to reduce the chance of suspension in the first place:

  • Make sure your name, address, and date of birth on the application match exactly what is on your ITIN assignment letter (CP-565).
  • Have at least 6 months of reported credit history before signing up; thinner files trigger more verification flags.
  • Use a U.S. IP address if signing up from abroad.

How does monitoring your ITIN credit score affect the score itself?

It does not. Checking your own credit through any of the services in this guide counts as a soft inquiry and does not lower your score. Only hard inquiries, the kind a lender initiates when you apply for credit, carry a temporary point deduction. You can check your score every single day through Equifax Core Credit and it will have zero effect.

For a deeper look at how hard vs. soft pulls affect your ITIN credit file, see our guide on hard inquiries with an ITIN.

How large is the ITIN population that needs these services?

The demand for ITIN-compatible monitoring is substantial and growing. More than 31 million ITINs have been issued since their introduction, with approximately 5 million currently active as of March 2026. In calendar year 2025 alone, 469,888 new ITINs were issued.

Despite that scale, until recently two of the three major credit bureaus required ITIN-based credit information requests to be submitted through traditional mail, leaving millions of people unable to check their own credit files online. The April 2026 MyFreeScoreNow launch is a meaningful step toward parity, but as the table at the top of this article shows, many gaps remain. TransUnion still has no free standalone online score dashboard for ITIN holders, and Credit Sesame’s ITIN support is inconsistently reported.

The tools that work today are better than they were two years ago, but they require more setup effort than the SSN-based equivalents. A one-time investment of 30-60 minutes to configure Equifax Core Credit plus myFICO Free gives you a solid ongoing monitoring baseline at zero monthly cost.
