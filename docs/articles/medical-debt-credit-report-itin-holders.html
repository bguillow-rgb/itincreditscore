Medical Debt & Your ITIN Credit Report (2026 Guide)

By Editorial Staff · Credit Reports & Bureaus · Published September 2, 2026

Quick Answer

Yes, medical debt can affect your ITIN credit report and credit score in 2026, but major voluntary bureau changes mean paid medical collections and any unpaid balance under $500 no longer appear. Unpaid medical collections over $500 can still show up and drag down your score, but newer scoring models already weight them less heavily.

Medical debt is one of the most unpredictable financial surprises an ITIN holder can face: an emergency room visit, an unexpected specialist bill, a statement buried in the mail that eventually lands in collections. The good news in 2026 is that bureau rules have shifted considerably in your favor. The complicated news is that the sweeping federal protection many consumers counted on was struck down, leaving a patchwork that is genuinely confusing. This guide explains exactly where things stand and what you can do.

Does medical debt actually show up on an ITIN credit report?

A question we hear often: whether holding an ITIN (rather than an SSN) changes how medical debt is handled by the bureaus.

It does not. Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion build credit files around a combination of identifiers: name, address, date of birth, and taxpayer identification number. If you have an ITIN, you’ll need to submit a request in writing to get your Experian credit report, but the data inside that file follows the same rules as any other consumer. If a medical collection is reported to any of the three bureaus, it will attach to your ITIN credit file just as it would attach to an SSN file.

The practical risk for ITIN holders is that a thin credit file makes a single negative item more damaging. When you have only two or three accounts on record, one medical collection can dominate the payment history and derogatory marks categories that together drive a large portion of your score. That is why understanding what actually appears, and what no longer does, matters more for ITIN holders than for someone with 10 years of dense credit history.

What changed in 2026: what medical debt is (and isn’t) on your report now?

This is the section most people get wrong, so it is worth being precise.

In 2023, Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion voluntarily made the most consequential change to medical debt credit reporting in decades. Paid medical debt is no longer reported regardless of how long it took to pay, and unpaid medical collections under $500 are no longer reported even if they remain unpaid. A new medical debt also has a 365-day grace period before it can appear at all, giving you time to resolve billing disputes before a collection hits your file.

The CFPB estimated those voluntary changes removed about 70% of medical debt tradelines from U.S. credit reports. That is meaningful but imperfect: roughly 30% of medical debt tradelines remain. Medical debt over $500 that is in collections may still appear on your report, though it has been excluded from VantageScore 4.0 and FICO Score 9 and 10 calculations.

What happened to the CFPB rule that was supposed to remove all medical debt?

This one comes up a lot: many ITIN holders heard about a federal rule that would erase all medical debt from credit reports and are understandably confused about whether it is still in effect.

In January 2025, the CFPB finalized a rule that would have removed all medical debt from consumer credit reports nationwide and barred lenders from using medical debt information in underwriting decisions. Had it taken effect, the CFPB estimated it would have removed $49 billion in medical debt from the credit files of approximately 15 million Americans. The rule never took effect. On July 11, 2025, a U.S. district court vacated it.

The court found the CFPB exceeded its statutory authority under the Fair Credit Reporting Act, which gives consumers the right to dispute inaccurate information but does not prohibit reporting of certain debt types based on their origin. As of April 2026, the rule is no longer enforceable.

The consequence for ITIN holders: you cannot count on a federal ban. The three major credit bureaus voluntarily removed medical collections under $500 from reports in 2023 and removed paid medical collections regardless of amount, and these voluntary changes remain in effect. As of 2026, 15 states ban medical debt from credit reports outright. If you live in one of those states, state-level protection may still apply to you regardless of the federal rule’s status.

How much can a medical collection actually hurt my ITIN score?

Readers frequently ask: whether a medical bill in collections is really as damaging as, say, a missed credit card payment.

For a consumer with a long, established credit history, the damage from a single medical collection is real but often survivable. For an ITIN holder with a thin file, the math is harsher. Payment history accounts for 35% of a FICO score and roughly 40% of a VantageScore. When you have only a credit-builder loan and one secured card on record, a derogatory medical tradeline can immediately drop you into a range that makes future credit much harder to access.

The drop varies widely depending on your starting score and file thickness. Research cited by the CFPB found that medical collections are a poor predictor of whether a borrower will repay future debts, which is partly why newer scoring models penalize them less. But if your lender pulls a FICO Score 8 (still the most widely used model for credit cards and many loans), unpaid medical collections over $500 that are more than a year old will be fully counted. A single item can push a score down by 50 to 100 points or more on a thin file. That gap is the difference between qualifying for a credit-builder account and being declined.

The fastest path back is paying on time every month going forward, keeping utilization low, and disputing any medical entry that should have been removed under the bureau’s voluntary policies.

How do I dispute a medical collection on my ITIN credit report?

If you see a medical debt on your report that you believe should have been removed, you have rights under the Fair Credit Reporting Act regardless of whether you use an ITIN or SSN. Even with the CFPB rule gone, you still have the right to dispute inaccurate information under the FCRA.

Start by pulling your reports from all three bureaus. Because Experian requires ITIN holders to make written requests, send a certified letter. Paid medical collections should no longer appear on your report. Medical debts under $500 have been removed regardless of paid or unpaid status, and medical debts less than one year old are not reported.

If any of these appear on your report anyway, file a dispute with the specific bureau. Include your ITIN, a government-issued ID, and any supporting documentation: a receipt of payment, a balance statement showing the amount was under $500, or proof of the service date. The bureau is required to investigate and respond within 30 days. For a detailed walkthrough of the dispute process, see our guide on how to dispute credit report errors with an ITIN.

Medical debt reporting errors are more common than most consumers expect, particularly when bills pass through multiple insurers or third-party collectors. Always verify the amount and the date before assuming a collection is valid.

Does medical debt affect my ITIN credit score differently than other negative items?

It depends on the scoring model. Under FICO Score 8 (still the dominant model in 2026), a paid medical collection carries the same weight as a paid non-medical collection: once paid, the harm fades, but paid collections of any kind still show on a FICO 8 report. Under FICO Score 9, 10, and VantageScore 4.0, paid collections have zero impact and medical collections in general carry significantly less weight.

This is why the scoring model your lender uses matters in practice. If you are building credit with an ITIN and you know a lender uses a newer model, a medical collection may be far less damaging to your approval odds than you fear. If you are applying for a credit card from a major bank that still uses FICO 8, the collection will be visible and scored. Ask your lender which FICO version they pull. The answer is often available by calling the issuer’s credit department before you apply, which avoids a hard inquiry on a file that might not qualify yet.

For a fuller picture of how scoring models differ for ITIN holders, see FICO Score vs. VantageScore for ITIN holders.

Practical steps: what to do right now if you have medical debt

  1. Pull all three reports. Check Equifax and TransUnion online; contact Experian in writing. Look for any medical tradeline and note the date, amount, and paid or unpaid status.
  2. Check the $500 threshold. Any medical collection under $500 should already be gone. If it is still there, dispute it with documentation of the balance.
  3. Confirm paid status is reflected. If you paid a medical bill that went to collections, that entry should have been removed. A letter from the collector confirming payment is your strongest dispute document.
  4. Check your state. If you live in one of the 15 states with medical debt credit reporting protections, you may have additional grounds for removal regardless of the federal rule’s status.
  5. Monitor going forward. Use a credit monitoring service that accepts an ITIN so you see any new medical tradeline within days of it appearing, while you still have time to resolve it before it ages past the 365-day grace period.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can medical debt appear on my credit report if I only have an ITIN? Yes. The three credit bureaus report based on your credit file and its attached identifiers, not whether you hold an SSN or an ITIN. If a medical collection is reported to Equifax, Experian, or TransUnion, it attaches to your ITIN file just as it would to any other file.

What medical debts are no longer reported to the credit bureaus in 2026? As of 2026, all three major bureaus voluntarily removed paid medical collections (any amount), unpaid medical collections under $500, and any medical debt less than one year old. These changes remain in effect even though the CFPB rule was vacated.

Does the CFPB medical debt rule still protect ITIN holders in 2026? No. A federal court vacated the CFPB rule in July 2025, and it is no longer enforceable as of 2026. Protection now comes only from the bureaus’ voluntary policies and, for some consumers, state laws in the 15 states that passed their own medical debt credit reporting restrictions.

How many points can a medical collection lower my credit score with an ITIN? It depends on your overall file. For someone with a thin ITIN credit file, a single medical collection sent to a collector can drop a score by 50 to 100 points or more, since payment history is the most heavily weighted factor in both FICO and VantageScore models.

How do I dispute a medical collection on my ITIN credit report? Submit a written dispute to each bureau that shows the item, including your ITIN, a copy of your ID, and any proof the debt is paid, under $500, or less than one year old. Experian in particular requires ITIN holders to submit credit report requests and disputes in writing by mail.

Will paying off a medical collection improve my ITIN credit score right away? Under FICO Score 9, 10, and VantageScore 4.0, a paid medical collection is completely ignored in the score calculation, so paying it removes the scoring penalty immediately once the bureaus update your file. Under the older FICO Score 8, a paid collection has less impact than an unpaid one but still appears in your report. The improvement you see depends entirely on which model your lender uses.
